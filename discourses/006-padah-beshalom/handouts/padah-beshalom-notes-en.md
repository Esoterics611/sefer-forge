# He Redeemed My Soul in Peace

[The Mittler Rebbe’s discourse *Padah beShalom* · Sha’arei Teshuvah, Gate of Prayer, 49a–56d · a summary and chapter notes]{.subtitle}

[Each quotation is translated from the discourse; the footnote gives its exact words, with the chapter and the folio and column of the printed edition (49a = folio 49, first column). Abbreviations are left as printed.]{.note}

## The discourse in brief

The verse “He redeemed my soul in peace from the battle against me, for among the many they were with me”[ch. 1, 49a: [פדה בשלום נפשי מקרב לי כי ברבים היו עמדי]{.he}]{.fn} (Psalms 55:19) is expounded here on the service of prayer. The Zohar calls prayer “a time of battle”[ch. 2, 49c: [שעת קרבא]{.he}]{.fn}: the divine soul wages war on the animal soul in order to turn it, too, to the will of G‑d. The discourse explains what this war is, why stray thoughts arise precisely in prayer, and where the war reaches its limit: even when it succeeds, it works only “for the moment”[ch. 7, 53b: [לפי שעה]{.he}]{.fn}, and afterwards the foreign will “returns and reawakens”[ch. 7, 53a: [חוזר וניעור]{.he}]{.fn}.

Above the war there is another way, “redemption in peace”[ch. 11, 55c: [פדה בשלום]{.he}]{.fn}: the surrender of the soul itself, from the level of the *Yechidah*, the innermost point of the soul. There the evil is not subdued by war. It is “nullified in its essence”[ch. 11, 55c: [בטל בעצם]{.he}]{.fn}; the nullification does not change, and the person feels no pain in it at all. This is the peace that is above war, like what will be in the time to come, and it is the meaning of “with all your might”[ch. 11, 55c: [בכל מאדך]{.he}]{.fn}. The end of the verse, “for among the many they were with me”[ch. 1, 49a: [כי ברבים היו עמדי]{.he}]{.fn}, gives the reason: the light of the *Yechidah* is the general surrounding light, in which all of Israel are equal as one, and it shines in the singular few of every generation.

## Chapter by chapter

**Chapter 1 — The analogy of the fighters.**

- Two opposites are far apart by nature, “just as fire and water can never join together”[ch. 1, 49a: [כמו שלא יוכלו אש ומים להתחבר יחד]{.he}]{.fn}. Only when they fight do they draw close and cling, and this is “wrestling”[ch. 1, 49a: [התאבקות]{.he}]{.fn}, as in “and a man wrestled with him”[ch. 1, 49a: [ויאבק איש עמו]{.he}]{.fn}.
- This closeness is not real in itself. It comes “only for the sake of the war and the victory”[ch. 1, 49a: [רק למען המלחמה והניצוח]{.he}]{.fn}. And the aim of the war is that the defeated be absorbed into the victor: “the main intent of the war, in this wrestling and closeness, is their inclusion in one another”[ch. 1, 49c: [שעיקר המכוון מן המלחמה באיבוק וקירוב זה הוא בחי' ההתכללות זב"ז]{.he}]{.fn}, like fire that boils water until it is gone.
- The analogy is to the two souls. “The spirit of man is the one that rises”[ch. 1, 49c: [רוח האדם הוא העולה]{.he}]{.fn} and “the spirit of the beast goes down below”[ch. 1, 49c: [רוח הבהמה יורדת למטה]{.he}]{.fn}. When they are not fighting they are far from each other; in time of war they are “pressed together and clinging to one another”[ch. 1, 49c: [נצמדים ודבוקים זה עם זה]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 2 — Two levels in prayer.**

- “The time of prayer is a time of battle”[ch. 2, 49c: [דשעת צלות' שעת קרבא]{.he}]{.fn}. But there is a prayer that is no war at all: the divine soul rises by its own nature, “like the nullification and inclusion of a candle before a torch”[ch. 2, 49c: [כביטול והתכללות הנר בפני האבוקה]{.he}]{.fn}, as in the Shabbat prayer, “which is only to delight in G‑d”[ch. 2, 49c: [שהוא רק להתענג על ה']{.he}]{.fn}. The animal soul stays where it is.
- The second level is battle and war: the spirit of man wants “to turn even the spirit of the beast that is in the physical body”[ch. 2, 49d: [להפוך גם לרוח הבהמה שבגוף החומרי]{.he}]{.fn}, until there is “one heart for one’s Father in heaven”[ch. 2, 49d: [לב א' לאביו שבשמי']{.he}]{.fn}.
- Hence the question: a person prays with intent, and immediately afterwards “goes and busies himself with the vanities of the world”[ch. 2, 49d: [הולך ומתעסק בהבלי עולם]{.he}]{.fn}. The answer: the prayer is true on the side of the divine soul, and this is “the lip of truth shall be established forever”[ch. 2, 49d: [שפת אמת תכון לעד]{.he}]{.fn}. But the animal soul did not move from its place, and of it is said “but a lying tongue is only for a moment”[ch. 2, 49d: [ועד ארגיעה לשון שקר]{.he}]{.fn}. Only when the animal soul is defeated and included, “then of it too it is said: the lip of truth shall be established forever”[ch. 2, 50a: [אז גם בה נאמר שפת אמת תכון לעד]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 3 — Stray thoughts.**

- “This is the root reason why stray thoughts grow strong precisely at the time of prayer”[ch. 3, 50a: [וזהו שרש הטעם להתגברות המ"ז בשעת התפלה דווקא]{.he}]{.fn}: in time of war each side fights with all its strength. The animal soul draws close and clings to the divine soul “to confuse it with stray thoughts”[ch. 3, 50a: [לבלבלה במחשבות זרות]{.he}]{.fn}. When the divine soul does not seek to fight, as in the Shabbat prayer, “then stray thoughts do not grow strong at all”[ch. 3, 50b: [אז אין מתגברין מ"ז כלל]{.he}]{.fn}.
- A thought does not perish: “even a single thought that a person thinks is never lost”[ch. 3, 50b: [אפי' מחשב' אחת שאדם חושב אין לה איבוד]{.he}]{.fn}, and it leaves the body together with the soul.
- And a light cannot exist without a vessel. So many stray thoughts gather and “become the light and life of a complete soul”[ch. 3, 50b: [נעשים בחי' אור וחיות נפש שלמה]{.he}]{.fn}, destructive forces and accusers “that pursue the soul of the person”[ch. 3, 50c: [שרודפין לנפש האדם]{.he}]{.fn}. This is why, at the time of prayer, it is as if “his enemies and those who hate him rise against him”[ch. 3, 50c: [שקמים עליו אויביו ושונאיו]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 4 — “For behold, Your enemies, G‑d.”**

- The Name *Havayah* shines in the divine soul, and the stray thoughts are “the enemies of G‑d”[ch. 4, 50c: [אויבי ה']{.he}]{.fn}. Precisely in prayer they receive “added nourishment and life from the G‑dly light in the divine soul”[ch. 4, 50c: [תו' יניקה וחיות מאור אלקי שבנה"א]{.he}]{.fn}, for evil draws from the waste of the good, and “wherever there is much good, there the evil is found close and clinging”[ch. 4, 50c: [כל מקום שיש ריבוי הטוב שם נמצא קירוב ודיבוק הרע]{.he}]{.fn}. As “whoever is greater than his fellow, his inclination is greater”[ch. 4, 50c: [כל הגדול מחברו יצרו גדול]{.he}]{.fn}, and as thieves who come “precisely where there is a great treasure”[ch. 4, 50c: [במקום שיש אוצר רב דוקא]{.he}]{.fn}.
- The victor, too, is soiled: “whoever wrestles with a filthy one becomes filthy too”[ch. 4, 50d: [כל המתאבק עם מנוול מתנוול גם הוא]{.he}]{.fn}. Jacob defeated the angel of Esau, and still he “was soiled by this dust”[ch. 4, 50d: [נתלכלך מאבק זה]{.he}]{.fn}. So in every refinement: “the one who refines must soil his clothes”[ch. 4, 50d: [דמברר צריך להתלכלך בגדיו]{.he}]{.fn}, like the priest who removes the ashes from the altar in lesser garments.
- The pause in the cantillation between “Your enemies” and G‑d’s Name shows that this clinging is in truth “a complete break and separation”[ch. 4, 51a: [הפסק ופירוד גמור]{.he}]{.fn}. But their nourishment “is only for the moment”[ch. 4, 51a: [אינו רק לפי שעה בלבד]{.he}]{.fn}. After the victory the root of their life is cut off, “and this is what it says: for behold, Your enemies shall perish”[ch. 4, 51a: [וז"ש כי הנה אויביך יאבדו]{.he}]{.fn}, and “he swallowed wealth and shall vomit it up”[ch. 4, 51a: [חיל בלע ויקיאנו]{.he}]{.fn}.
- “Shall perish” is said of the stray thoughts “that did not come into bodies”[ch. 4, 51c: [שלא באו בגופי']{.he}]{.fn}. “Shall be scattered” is said of those already clothed in bodies: they are dispersed, and none has the strength to stand opposed.
- All this is “precisely now, when prayer is called battle”[ch. 4, 51c: [דוקא עכשיו דצלותא נק' קרבא]{.he}]{.fn}. In the time to come “they shall not harm nor destroy”[ch. 4, 51c: [לא ירעו ולא ישחיתו]{.he}]{.fn}, and there will be no war at all.

**Chapter 5 — Inner light and surrounding light.**

- War “applies only to the part of the divine soul that is clothed in the body”[ch. 5, 51c: [לא שייך רק בחלק של נה"א המלובש בגוף]{.he}]{.fn}, which is *Nefesh, Ruach* and *Neshamah*, inner light, “the love of ‘with all your heart and with all your soul’”[ch. 5, 51c: [אהבה דבכל לבבך ובכל נפשך]{.he}]{.fn}. But *Chayah* and *Yechidah* are “only as a surrounding light”[ch. 5, 51c: [רק בבחי' אור מקיף]{.he}]{.fn}, and in them, in the great love “with all your might,” “one cannot speak of war at all”[ch. 5, 51c: [לא שייך לומר בחי' מלחמה כלל]{.he}]{.fn}.
- The analogy is the life of the soul in the body. The inner light divides according to the limbs: in the brain, intellect; in the eye, sight; and so down to “the toenail”[ch. 5, 51d: [ציפורן הרגל]{.he}]{.fn}. The surrounding light is “the part of the soul that is its essence, which is the core and the source”[ch. 5, 51d: [חלק העצמות שבנפש שהוא העיקר והמקור]{.he}]{.fn}, and it shines over the whole body, head and foot, “in one and exactly the same measure”[ch. 5, 51d: [בהשוואה א' ממש]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 6 — Subduing and transforming.**

- In the *Yechidah* “actual G‑dliness dwells, and the external forces receive no nourishment there at all”[ch. 6, 52a: [שורה אלקות ממש ואין חיצונים מקבלים שם יניקה כלל]{.he}]{.fn}, and of it is said “my only one, from the hand of the dog”[ch. 6, 52a: [מיד כלב יחידתי]{.he}]{.fn}. There are “two surrounding lights, *Chayah* and *Yechidah*”[ch. 6, 52a: [ב' מקיפין בחי' חי' ובחי' יחידה]{.he}]{.fn}, and two kinds of will to match them. The will of *Chayah* shines in the mind and the heart. The second will is “a simple will, entirely above shining and spreading into the mind and heart”[ch. 6, 52a: [רצון פשוט שלמעלה מהארה והתפשטות לגמרי במוח ולב]{.he}]{.fn}.
- A small will gives way before a greater one. So a person gives up money for honour, or to save his life. But the small will is not transformed: “it is there in full force”[ch. 6, 52a: [ישנה בתקפה]{.he}]{.fn}, only it does not shine. “And this is called subdued and not transformed”[ch. 6, 52b: [וזהו נקרא אכפייא ולא אתהפכא]{.he}]{.fn}.
- Fire and water, by contrast, are transformed in their essence, “and this is the essence of true inclusion”[ch. 6, 52b: [וזהו עיקר בחי' ההתכללו' האמיתי]{.he}]{.fn}. Michael and Gabriel “do not extinguish one another”[ch. 6, 52c: [אין מכבים זה את זה]{.he}]{.fn}, but each only bears its opposite, like two rival ministers before the king who tolerate each other “from awe of the king”[ch. 6, 52c: [מאימת המלך]{.he}]{.fn}.
- “True peace, in true inclusion”[ch. 6, 52c: [שלום אמיתי בהתכללות אמיתי]{.he}]{.fn} comes in two ways: through war, “as the wars of Joshua and David”[ch. 6, 52c: [כמו מלחמות יהושע ודוד]{.he}]{.fn}, or without war, “as in the days of Solomon and the days of Moshiach”[ch. 6, 52c: [כמו בימי שלמה וימות המשיח]{.he}]{.fn}. Both kinds of war, subduing and the transformation of the essence, are in the inner light, and also in the *Chayah* that shines in mind and heart, “which is not so of the level of *Yechidah*”[ch. 6, 52c: [משא"כ בבחי' יחידה]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 7 — The surrender of the will.**

- “With all your heart” is “with both your inclinations”[ch. 7, 52c: [בשני יצרך]{.he}]{.fn}: subduing or transforming. It is done “through ‘with all your soul’, the surrounding light of *Chayah*”[ch. 7, 52c: [ע"י בכל נפשך בחי' המקיף דחי']{.he}]{.fn}, at the time of prayer. “With all your might” is the surrounding light of *Yechidah*, “which never shines in the mind and heart”[ch. 7, 52d: [שאינו מאיר לעולם במוחא ולבא]{.he}]{.fn}, and is “without limit in its essence”[ch. 7, 52d: [בלי גבול בעצם]{.he}]{.fn}. “And this is the true self-sacrifice, that comes after the self-sacrifice of ‘with all your soul’”[ch. 7, 52d: [וזהו בחי' מס"נ האמיתי שאחר מס"נ בכל נפשך]{.he}]{.fn}.
- Even the transformation of the foreign will at the time of prayer is “the nullification of the minority in the majority”[ch. 7, 52d: [ביטול המיעוט ברוב]{.he}]{.fn}: like a drop in the sea, bitter water in sweet water, radish in honey, “and he cast in a tree, and the waters were sweetened”[ch. 7, 53a: [וישלך עץ וימתקו המים]{.he}]{.fn}.
- So it holds only while the greater force is there: “as soon as the greater light and force withdraws … then it returns and reawakens”[ch. 7, 53a: [מיד כשיסתלק האור והכח הגדול... אזי חוזר וניעור]{.he}]{.fn}. And like wood that has burned: if the parts of fire are drawn out of it, “all the parts of water will be found in it, whole”[ch. 7, 53b: [ימצאו בו מציאות כל חלקי המים בשלימות]{.he}]{.fn}.
- Nevertheless, this is self-sacrifice. But it is “the surrender of the will alone”[ch. 7, 53b: [מסירת הרצון לבד]{.he}]{.fn}, like something “handed over from one domain to another”[ch. 7, 53b: [הנמסר מרשות לרשות]{.he}]{.fn}: from the public domain, the world of separation, to the private domain, the world of unity. And what was handed over can undergo “a change of domain”[ch. 7, 53b: [להיות שינוי הרשות]{.he}]{.fn}, back again.

**Chapter 8 — The surrender of the soul itself; equanimity.**

- Above the surrender of the will there is “the surrender of the soul itself, specifically”[ch. 8, 53b: [מסירת הנפש בעצמה דוקא]{.he}]{.fn}. The soul itself “is entirely without will”[ch. 8, 53c: [מושללת מבחי' רצון לגמרי]{.he}]{.fn}, and to it “a great will and a small will are equal”[ch. 8, 53c: [שוה רצון הגדול עם רצון הקטן]{.he}]{.fn}, so there is no war of will against will in it. “This is nullification in essence, from its own side”[ch. 8, 53c: [זהו בחי' ביטול בעצם מצד עצמו]{.he}]{.fn}, and it is “the love of ‘with all your might’”[ch. 8, 53c: [אהבה דבכל מאדך]{.he}]{.fn}.
- The preface is the matter of equanimity. “The core and source that includes everything in the world is two things, pleasure and pain”[ch. 8, 53c: [העיקר והמקור הכולל כל הדברים שבעולם הם ב' דברים עונג וצער]{.he}]{.fn}. Pain is the reverse of pleasure, and comes “in one and the same measure”[ch. 8, 54a: [במדה ושיעור א']{.he}]{.fn} as it. And pain reaches the essence of the soul, “a broken spirit”[ch. 8, 53d: [רוח נשברה]{.he}]{.fn}, “for the letters of *nega* (affliction) are those of *oneg* (pleasure)”[ch. 8, 53d: [דנגע אותיות עונג]{.he}]{.fn}.
- Hence the test: one who is pained by a loss of money or a blow to his honour necessarily “has will and pleasure in money and honour”[ch. 8, 54a: [שיש לו רצון ותענוג בממון וכבוד]{.he}]{.fn}. And there are complete tzaddikim who are not pained at all, “for whom yes and no are exactly equal”[ch. 8, 54b: [ששוה אצלו הן ולאו ממש]{.he}]{.fn}, as “G‑d gave and G‑d has taken”[ch. 8, 54b: [ה' נתן וה' לקח]{.he}]{.fn}; therefore one blesses “over the bad just as one blesses over the good”[ch. 8, 54b: [על הרעה כמו שמברך על הטובה]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 9 — The root of equanimity: the Yechidah.**

- Equanimity comes because there has been taken away “the root and source of the life of the animal soul”[ch. 9, 54b: [שורש ומקור חיי נפש הבהמית]{.he}]{.fn}, “the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* that are in the animal soul”[ch. 9, 54b: [בחי' חיה יחידה שבנה"ב]{.he}]{.fn}.
- As long as the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* of the animal soul still shine, the service is a “trial”[ch. 9, 54b: [נסיון]{.he}]{.fn}, as “and G‑d tested Abraham”[ch. 9, 54b: [והאלקים נסה את אברהם]{.he}]{.fn}, and “it can be one thing or its opposite”[ch. 9, 54c: [יוכל להיות דבר והיפוכו]{.he}]{.fn}. Above this is “a settled thing, in which there is no doubt at all”[ch. 9, 54c: [דבר מוחלט שאין בו ספק כלל]{.he}]{.fn}, which needs no trial.
- “My only one, from the hand of the dog”[ch. 9, 54c: [מיד כלב יחידתי]{.he}]{.fn}: the *Yechidah* of the divine soul transforms the *Yechidah* of the animal soul, “not by way of war and victory at all, but of itself”[ch. 9, 54c: [ולא מצד מלחמה ונצוח כלל אלא ממילא]{.he}]{.fn}. This is “nullification in essence”[ch. 9, 54c: [ביטול בעצם]{.he}]{.fn}, “always in equanimity, without change”[ch. 9, 54c: [תמיד בהשוואה בלי שינוי]{.he}]{.fn}, “the arousal of the essence”[ch. 9, 54c: [התפעלו' העצמית]{.he}]{.fn}, “my soul is consumed”[ch. 9, 54c: [כלתה נפשי]{.he}]{.fn}, “all my bones shall say”[ch. 9, 54c: [כל עצמותי תאמרנה]{.he}]{.fn} (*atzmotai*, “my bones,” read as “my essence”), and as “with one knot I am bound”[ch. 9, 54d: [בחד קטירא אתקטרנא]{.he}]{.fn}. Such was the nullification of the Patriarchs, who were the chariot, and such was “we will do and we will hear”[ch. 9, 54d: [נעשה ונשמע]{.he}]{.fn} at the giving of the Torah.
- The proof is the eve of Yom Kippur. Even “the lowest of the low, who transgressed the whole Torah”[ch. 9, 54d: [קל שבקלי' שעבר על כל התורה]{.he}]{.fn} weeps in repentance, “because of the essential *Yechidah* in him, which was not defiled by the impurity of his sins”[ch. 9, 54d: [מצד בחי' יחידה העצמית שבו שלא הוטמאה בטומאת עונות שלו]{.he}]{.fn}.
- One who serves from the *Yechidah* needs no war even after prayer, “as he walks all day in the marketplace”[ch. 9, 54d: [בהלוכו כל היום בשוק]{.he}]{.fn}. Of him it is said “in the valley of the shadow of death I will fear no evil”[ch. 9, 54d: [בגיא צלמות לא אירא רע]{.he}]{.fn}, and he is “in the very image of G‑d”[ch. 9, 55a: [בצלם אלקים ממש]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 10 — The analogy of friends.**

- A faithful friend gives himself to save his friend, and in this there are levels. One bears the pain “by way of endurance”[ch. 10, 55b: [דרך סבלנות]{.he}]{.fn} and feels it greatly. Another does not feel it so much, by way of “the nullification of the minority in the majority”[ch. 10, 55b: [ביטול המיעוט ברוב]{.he}]{.fn}, and this too “immediately returns and reawakens”[ch. 10, 55b: [מיד חוזר וניער]{.he}]{.fn}.
- But when his soul is bound to his friend’s soul in its essence, “like the love of David and Jonathan”[ch. 10, 55b: [כאהבת דוד ויהונתן]{.he}]{.fn}, “as if they were truly one soul”[ch. 10, 55b: [כאלו הן נפש א' ממש]{.he}]{.fn}, “he will feel no suffering whatsoever”[ch. 10, 55b: [לא ירגיש כלל וכלל ביסורים]{.he}]{.fn}. On the contrary, all is done “in the way of love, pleasure and great joy”[ch. 10, 55b: [דרך אהבה ותענוג ושמחה גדולה]{.he}]{.fn}.
- So with “with all your might”: the nullification of the foreign will there “is not called suffering, but pleasure and joy”[ch. 10, 55c: [לא נק' יסורי' אלא תענוג ושמחה]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 11 — “He redeemed my soul in peace.”**

- “My soul”[ch. 11, 55c: [נפשי]{.he}]{.fn} is the divine soul, and it has two levels. The first is the surrender of the will, “by way of war and victory, as at the time of prayer”[ch. 11, 55c: [דרך מלחמה וניצוח כמו בשעת צלותא]{.he}]{.fn}. The second is “with all your might”[ch. 11, 55c: [בכל מאדך]{.he}]{.fn}, the surrender of the soul itself, “which is entirely above war”[ch. 11, 55c: [שלמעלה מבחי' מלחמה לגמרי]{.he}]{.fn}. This is the *Yechidah*, “through which it has redemption in peace, without war at all”[ch. 11, 55c: [שע"י יש לה פדיה בשלום בלא מלחמה כלל]{.he}]{.fn}.
- “Peace is one who sits in tranquility, with no opponent at all”[ch. 11, 55c: [שלום הוא היושב בשלוה שאין לו מנגד כלל]{.he}]{.fn}. In war even the divine soul is not at peace, for “even the toil of this war”[ch. 11, 55d: [גם טורח מלחמה זאת]{.he}]{.fn} counts for it as suffering; and it is also soiled a little by the garments of the animal soul.
- This peace is like the time to come: “the lion shall eat straw like the ox”[ch. 11, 55d: [ארי' כבקר יאכל תבן]{.he}]{.fn}, “and all the nations shall stream to it”[ch. 11, 55d: [ונהרו אליו כל הגוים]{.he}]{.fn}, “and not as in the days of David and Joshua, who conquered by war”[ch. 11, 55d: [ולא כמו בימי דוד ויהושע שכבשו ע"י מלחמה]{.he}]{.fn}. David said “He redeemed my soul in peace”[ch. 11, 55c: [פדה בשלום נפשי]{.he}]{.fn} of his soul as it will be in the time to come.
- In war the enemies remain “enemies of G‑d”[ch. 11, 56a: [אויבי ה']{.he}]{.fn}, and they perish. In the way of peace “they become complete friends”[ch. 11, 56a: [נעשים אוהבים גמורים]{.he}]{.fn}, like Esau, of whom it is written “and he ran … toward him and kissed him”[ch. 11, 56a: [וירץ... לקראתו וישקהו]{.he}]{.fn}.
- And why is redemption needed, if there is no opponent? “Because of the root of the hold of the *kelipah* on the side of holiness, a ransom must be given them, a small thing in exchange for a great one”[ch. 11, 56a: [מצד שורש האחיזה דקליפה בסיטרא דקדושה צריך ליתן להם פדיון שהוא דבר קטן תמורת גדול]{.he}]{.fn}, like the ransom of a soul, the Hebrew maidservant, and the goat sent away on Yom Kippur. The redemption is “so that the animal soul will not join the divine soul as it rises above”[ch. 11, 56b: [כדי שלא תתחבר הנה"ב עם הנ"א בעלותה למעלה]{.he}]{.fn}.
- The true example is the Ten Martyrs. Rabbi Akiva drew out the word “One” even under iron combs, and “did not feel it as suffering at all”[ch. 11, 56b: [לא הרגיש לבחי' יסורים כלל]{.he}]{.fn}. This is “when will it come to my hand, that I may fulfil it”[ch. 11, 56b: [מתי יבא לידי ואקיימנו]{.he}]{.fn}. “Their self-sacrifice was in joy and pleasure”[ch. 11, 56b: [מס"נ שלהם היה בשמחה ותענוג]{.he}]{.fn}.
- “From the battle against me”[ch. 11, 56b: [מקרב לי]{.he}]{.fn} is “a word of closeness and a word of war”[ch. 11, 56b: [לשון קירוב ולשון מלחמה]{.he}]{.fn}: that there be no battle. In peace there is “a true closeness, like faithful friends”[ch. 11, 56b: [קירוב אמיתי כאוהבים נאמנים]{.he}]{.fn}, and after it the divine soul is separated “through redemption”[ch. 11, 56c: [ע"י פדיה]{.he}]{.fn}, as “please separate from me”[ch. 11, 56c: [הפרד נא מעלי]{.he}]{.fn}.

**Chapter 12 — “For among the many they were with me.”**

- This is the reason for redemption in peace. “Among the many” is “the surrounding light of *Yechidah*, which is called the general surrounding light”[ch. 12, 56c: [המקיף דיחידה שנק' מקיף כללי]{.he}]{.fn}, what is hidden within the many, as “and Israel camped (*vayichan*), in the singular”[ch. 12, 56c: [ויחן ישראל לשון יחיד]{.he}]{.fn}. In the *Chayah* there are six hundred thousand understandings, “each according to his grasp”[ch. 12, 56c: [כל אחד לפי השגתו]{.he}]{.fn}. But in the *Yechidah* “all are equal, and are called one man”[ch. 12, 56c: [כולם שוים ונק' איש אחד]{.he}]{.fn}.
- “Was with me”[ch. 12, 56c: [היה עמדי]{.he}]{.fn}, in the singular and in revelation: this general light “shines in the singular few of every generation”[ch. 12, 56c: [מאיר ביחידי סגולה שבכל דור]{.he}]{.fn}. They are Moses, of whom it says “in whose midst I am”[ch. 12, 56c: [אשר אנכי בקרבו]{.he}]{.fn}, the Ten Martyrs, and the thirty-six tzaddikim “who see the face of the Shechinah every day”[ch. 12, 56d: [דחזו אפי שכינתא בכל יומא]{.he}]{.fn}, “the eyes of the congregation”[ch. 12, 56c: [עיני העדה]{.he}]{.fn}, who include them all.
- And in its plain sense: “in every gathering of ten”[ch. 12, 56d: [אכל בי עשרה]{.he}]{.fn} the Shechinah dwells, “and even Moses needed to pray with ten”[ch. 12, 56d: [ואפי' משה היה צריך להתפלל בעשרה]{.he}]{.fn}.
- This surrounding light is revealed in the inwardness of Wisdom, “the nullification of being into nothing, as it is, as actual nothing”[ch. 12, 56d: [ביטול היש לאין כמו שהוא בבחי' אין ממש]{.he}]{.fn}. Therefore “even in contemplation the nullification of being into nothing is grasped”[ch. 12, 56d: [גם בהתבוננות נתפס בחי' ביטול היש לאין]{.he}]{.fn}, “which is not so in the love of ‘with all your soul’, which is the surrounding light of *Chayah* alone”[ch. 12, 56d: [משא"כ באהבה דבכל נפשך שהוא מקיף דחי' לבד]{.he}]{.fn}.
