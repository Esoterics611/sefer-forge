# He Redeemed My Soul in Peace

[A discourse of the Mitteler Rebbe, Rabbi DovBer of Lubavitch · *Sha’arei Teshuvah*, Gate of Prayer, 49a–56d · with an explanation]{.subtitle}

[[SEFER FORGE]{.wordmark} · The inner Torah, explained and taught · esoterics611.github.io]{.brand}

## Introduction

[Introduction.1]{.pnum} The discourse *Padah beShalom* is printed in *Sha’arei Teshuvah* by the Mitteler Rebbe, Rabbi DovBer of Lubavitch, in the Gate of Prayer, under the heading “An explanation of the foundation of prayer.” It is built on a verse in Psalms: “He redeemed my soul in peace from the battle against me, for among the many they were with me” (Psalms 55:19).

[Introduction.2]{.pnum} The discourse expounds the verse on the service of prayer. The Zohar calls prayer “a time of battle”: the divine soul wages war on the animal soul in order to turn it, too, to the will of G‑d. The discourse explains what this war is, why stray thoughts arise precisely in prayer, and where the war reaches its limit: even when it succeeds, it works only “for the moment”, and afterwards the foreign will “returns and reawakens”.

[Introduction.3]{.pnum} Above the war there is another way, “redemption in peace”: the surrender of the soul itself, from the level of the *Yechidah*, the innermost point of the soul. There the evil is not subdued by war. It is “nullified in its essence”; the nullification does not change, and the person feels no pain in it at all. This is the peace that is above war, like what will be in the time to come, and it is the meaning of “with all your might”. The end of the verse, “for among the many they were with me”, gives the reason: the light of the *Yechidah* is the general surrounding light, in which all of Israel are equal as one, and it shines in the singular few of every generation.

[Introduction.4]{.pnum} Along the way the discourse explains several foundations: the difference between inner light and surrounding light, and between *Chayah* and *Yechidah*; subduing and transforming (*itkafya* and *ithapcha*); the surrender of the will and the surrender of the soul itself; equanimity, tested by pleasure and pain; and trial, and what is above it.

### The four levels in the verse “And you shall love”

Chapter 9 reads the verse as a ladder. Each level is described in the discourse’s own words:
::::

| In the verse | In the soul | The way | How long |
|---|---|---|---|
| “And you shall love” | “a word of desire, which is the will shining openly in mind and heart” | inner light, “in limitation within a vessel” | — |
| “with all your heart” | “with both your inclinations” | “to subdue the animal soul” | — |
| “with all your soul” | “the surrounding light of *Chayah*” | “transforming, the nullification of the essence”, war and victory, or trial | “for the moment … and afterwards it can return” |
| “with all your might” | “the level of *Yechidah*, which does not shine even as a surrounding light to the inner” | nullification in essence, without war: “redemption in peace” | “it never has change or fall” |
:::

### The chapters in brief

#### Chapter 1: The analogy of the fighters

- [Introduction.5]{.pnum} Two opposites are far apart by nature, “just as fire and water can never join together”. Only when they fight do they draw close and cling, and this is “wrestling”, as in “and a man wrestled with him”.
- [Introduction.6]{.pnum} This closeness is not real in itself. It comes “only for the sake of the war and the victory”. And the aim of the war is that the defeated be absorbed into the victor: “the main intent of the war, in this wrestling and closeness, is their inclusion in one another”, like fire that boils water until it is gone.
- [Introduction.7]{.pnum} The analogy is to the two souls. “The spirit of man is the one that rises” and “the spirit of the beast goes down below”. When they are not fighting they are far from each other; in time of war they are “pressed together and clinging to one another”.

#### Chapter 2: Two levels in prayer

- [Introduction.8]{.pnum} “The time of prayer is a time of battle”. But there is a prayer that is no war at all: the divine soul rises by its own nature, “like the nullification and inclusion of a candle before a torch”, as in the Shabbat prayer, “which is only to delight in G‑d”. The animal soul stays where it is.
- [Introduction.9]{.pnum} The second level is battle and war: the spirit of man wants “to turn even the spirit of the beast that is in the physical body”, until there is “one heart for one’s Father in heaven”.
- [Introduction.10]{.pnum} Hence the question: a person prays with intent, and immediately afterwards “goes and busies himself with the vanities of the world”. The answer: the prayer is true on the side of the divine soul, and this is “the lip of truth shall be established forever”. But the animal soul did not move from its place, and of it is said “but a lying tongue is only for a moment”. Only when the animal soul is defeated and included, “then of it too it is said: the lip of truth shall be established forever”.

#### Chapter 3: Stray thoughts

- [Introduction.11]{.pnum} “This is the root reason why stray thoughts grow strong precisely at the time of prayer”: in time of war each side fights with all its strength. The animal soul draws close and clings to the divine soul “to confuse it with stray thoughts”. When the divine soul does not seek to fight, as in the Shabbat prayer, “then stray thoughts do not grow strong at all”.
- [Introduction.12]{.pnum} A thought does not perish: “even a single thought that a person thinks is never lost”, and it leaves the body together with the soul.
- [Introduction.13]{.pnum} And a light cannot exist without a vessel. So many stray thoughts gather and “become the light and life of a complete soul”, destructive forces and accusers “that pursue the soul of the person”. This is why, at the time of prayer, it is as if “his enemies and those who hate him rise against him”.

#### Chapter 4: “For behold, Your enemies, G‑d.”

- [Introduction.14]{.pnum} The Name *Havayah* shines in the divine soul, and the stray thoughts are “the enemies of G‑d”. Precisely in prayer they receive “added nourishment and life from the G‑dly light in the divine soul”, for evil draws from the waste of the good, and “wherever there is much good, there the evil is found close and clinging”. As “whoever is greater than his fellow, his inclination is greater”, and as thieves who come “precisely where there is a great treasure”.
- [Introduction.15]{.pnum} The victor, too, is soiled: “whoever wrestles with a filthy one becomes filthy too”. Jacob defeated the angel of Esau, and still he “was soiled by this dust”. So in every refinement: “the one who refines must soil his clothes”, like the priest who removes the ashes from the altar in lesser garments.
- [Introduction.16]{.pnum} The pause in the cantillation between “Your enemies” and G‑d’s Name shows that this clinging is in truth “a complete break and separation”. But their nourishment “is only for the moment”. After the victory the root of their life is cut off, “and this is what it says: for behold, Your enemies shall perish”, and “he swallowed wealth and shall vomit it up”.
- [Introduction.17]{.pnum} “Shall perish” is said of the stray thoughts “that did not come into bodies”. “Shall be scattered” is said of those already clothed in bodies: they are dispersed, and none has the strength to stand opposed.
- [Introduction.18]{.pnum} All this is “precisely now, when prayer is called battle”. In the time to come “they shall not harm nor destroy”, and there will be no war at all.

#### Chapter 5: Inner light and surrounding light

- [Introduction.19]{.pnum} War “applies only to the part of the divine soul that is clothed in the body”, which is *Nefesh, Ruach* and *Neshamah*, inner light, “the love of ‘with all your heart and with all your soul’”. But *Chayah* and *Yechidah* are “only as a surrounding light”, and in them, in the great love “with all your might,” “one cannot speak of war at all”.
- [Introduction.20]{.pnum} The analogy is the life of the soul in the body. The inner light divides according to the limbs: in the brain, intellect; in the eye, sight; and so down to “the toenail”. The surrounding light is “the part of the soul that is its essence, which is the core and the source”, and it shines over the whole body, head and foot, “in one and exactly the same measure”.

#### Chapter 6: Subduing and transforming

- [Introduction.21]{.pnum} In the *Yechidah* “actual G‑dliness dwells, and the external forces receive no nourishment there at all”, and of it is said “my only one, from the hand of the dog”. There are “two surrounding lights, *Chayah* and *Yechidah*”, and two kinds of will to match them. The will of *Chayah* shines in the mind and the heart. The second will is “a simple will, entirely above shining and spreading into the mind and heart”.
- [Introduction.22]{.pnum} A small will gives way before a greater one. So a person gives up money for honour, or to save his life. But the small will is not transformed: “it is there in full force”, only it does not shine. “And this is called subdued and not transformed”.
- [Introduction.23]{.pnum} Fire and water, by contrast, are transformed in their essence, “and this is the essence of true inclusion”. Michael and Gabriel “do not extinguish one another”, but each only bears its opposite, like two rival ministers before the king who tolerate each other “from awe of the king”.
- [Introduction.24]{.pnum} “True peace, in true inclusion” comes in two ways: through war, “as the wars of Joshua and David”, or without war, “as in the days of Solomon and the days of Moshiach”. Both kinds of war, subduing and the transformation of the essence, are in the inner light, and also in the *Chayah* that shines in mind and heart, “which is not so of the level of *Yechidah*”.

#### Chapter 7: The surrender of the will

- [Introduction.25]{.pnum} “With all your heart” is “with both your inclinations”: subduing or transforming. It is done “through ‘with all your soul’, the surrounding light of *Chayah*”, at the time of prayer. “With all your might” is the surrounding light of *Yechidah*, “which never shines in the mind and heart”, and is “without limit in its essence”. “And this is the true self-sacrifice, that comes after the self-sacrifice of ‘with all your soul’”.
- [Introduction.26]{.pnum} Even the transformation of the foreign will at the time of prayer is “the nullification of the minority in the majority”: like a drop in the sea, bitter water in sweet water, radish in honey, “and he cast in a tree, and the waters were sweetened”.
- [Introduction.27]{.pnum} So it holds only while the greater force is there: “as soon as the greater light and force withdraws … then it returns and reawakens”. And like wood that has burned: if the parts of fire are drawn out of it, “all the parts of water will be found in it, whole”.
- [Introduction.28]{.pnum} Nevertheless, this is self-sacrifice. But it is “the surrender of the will alone”, like something “handed over from one domain to another”: from the public domain, the world of separation, to the private domain, the world of unity. And what was handed over can undergo “a change of domain”, back again.

#### Chapter 8: The surrender of the soul itself; equanimity

- [Introduction.29]{.pnum} Above the surrender of the will there is “the surrender of the soul itself, specifically”. The soul itself “is entirely without will”, and to it “a great will and a small will are equal”, so there is no war of will against will in it. “This is nullification in essence, from its own side”, and it is “the love of ‘with all your might’”.
- [Introduction.30]{.pnum} The preface is the matter of equanimity. “The core and source that includes everything in the world is two things, pleasure and pain”. Pain is the reverse of pleasure, and comes “in one and the same measure” as it. And pain reaches the essence of the soul, “a broken spirit”, “for the letters of *nega* (affliction) are those of *oneg* (pleasure)”.
- [Introduction.31]{.pnum} Hence the test: one who is pained by a loss of money or a blow to his honour necessarily “has will and pleasure in money and honour”. And there are complete tzaddikim who are not pained at all, “for whom yes and no are exactly equal”, as “G‑d gave and G‑d has taken”; therefore one blesses “over the bad just as one blesses over the good”.

#### Chapter 9: The root of equanimity: the Yechidah

- [Introduction.32]{.pnum} Equanimity comes because there has been taken away “the root and source of the life of the animal soul”, “the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* that are in the animal soul”.
- [Introduction.33]{.pnum} As long as the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* of the animal soul still shine, the service is a “trial”, as “and G‑d tested Abraham”, and “it can be one thing or its opposite”. Above this is “a settled thing, in which there is no doubt at all”, which needs no trial.
- [Introduction.34]{.pnum} “My only one, from the hand of the dog”: the *Yechidah* of the divine soul transforms the *Yechidah* of the animal soul, “not by way of war and victory at all, but of itself”. This is “nullification in essence”, “always in equanimity, without change”, “the arousal of the essence”, “my soul is consumed”, “all my bones shall say” (*atzmotai*, “my bones,” read as “my essence”), and as “with one knot I am bound”. Such was the nullification of the Patriarchs, who were the chariot, and such was “we will do and we will hear” at the giving of the Torah.
- [Introduction.35]{.pnum} The proof is the eve of Yom Kippur. Even “the lowest of the low, who transgressed the whole Torah” weeps in repentance, “because of the essential *Yechidah* in him, which was not defiled by the impurity of his sins”.
- [Introduction.36]{.pnum} One who serves from the *Yechidah* needs no war even after prayer, “as he walks all day in the marketplace”. Of him it is said “in the valley of the shadow of death I will fear no evil”, and he is “in the very image of G‑d”.

#### Chapter 10: The analogy of friends

- [Introduction.37]{.pnum} A faithful friend gives himself to save his friend, and in this there are levels. One bears the pain “by way of endurance” and feels it greatly. Another does not feel it so much, by way of “the nullification of the minority in the majority”, and this too “immediately returns and reawakens”.
- [Introduction.38]{.pnum} But when his soul is bound to his friend’s soul in its essence, “like the love of David and Jonathan”, “as if they were truly one soul”, “he will feel no suffering whatsoever”. On the contrary, all is done “in the way of love, pleasure and great joy”.
- [Introduction.39]{.pnum} So with “with all your might”: the nullification of the foreign will there “is not called suffering, but pleasure and joy”.

#### Chapter 11: “He redeemed my soul in peace.”

- [Introduction.40]{.pnum} “My soul” is the divine soul, and it has two levels. The first is the surrender of the will, “by way of war and victory, as at the time of prayer”. The second is “with all your might”, the surrender of the soul itself, “which is entirely above war”. This is the *Yechidah*, “through which it has redemption in peace, without war at all”.
- [Introduction.41]{.pnum} “Peace is one who sits in tranquility, with no opponent at all”. In war even the divine soul is not at peace, for “even the toil of this war” counts for it as suffering; and it is also soiled a little by the garments of the animal soul.
- [Introduction.42]{.pnum} This peace is like the time to come: “the lion shall eat straw like the ox”, “and all the nations shall stream to it”, “and not as in the days of David and Joshua, who conquered by war”. David said “He redeemed my soul in peace” of his soul as it will be in the time to come.
- [Introduction.43]{.pnum} In war the enemies remain “enemies of G‑d”, and they perish. In the way of peace “they become complete friends”, like Esau, of whom it is written “and he ran … toward him and kissed him”.
- [Introduction.44]{.pnum} And why is redemption needed, if there is no opponent? “Because of the root of the hold of the *kelipah* on the side of holiness, a ransom must be given them, a small thing in exchange for a great one”, like the ransom of a soul, the Hebrew maidservant, and the goat sent away on Yom Kippur. The redemption is “so that the animal soul will not join the divine soul as it rises above”.
- [Introduction.45]{.pnum} The true example is the Ten Martyrs. Rabbi Akiva drew out the word “One” even under iron combs, and “did not feel it as suffering at all”. This is “when will it come to my hand, that I may fulfil it”. “Their self-sacrifice was in joy and pleasure”.
- [Introduction.46]{.pnum} “From the battle against me” is “a word of closeness and a word of war”: that there be no battle. In peace there is “a true closeness, like faithful friends”, and after it the divine soul is separated “through redemption”, as “please separate from me”.

#### Chapter 12: “For among the many they were with me.”

- [Introduction.47]{.pnum} This is the reason for redemption in peace. “Among the many” is “the surrounding light of *Yechidah*, which is called the general surrounding light”, what is hidden within the many, as “and Israel camped (*vayichan*), in the singular”. In the *Chayah* there are six hundred thousand understandings, “each according to his grasp”. But in the *Yechidah* “all are equal, and are called one man”.
- [Introduction.48]{.pnum} “Was with me”, in the singular and in revelation: this general light “shines in the singular few of every generation”. They are Moses, of whom it says “in whose midst I am”, the Ten Martyrs, and the thirty-six tzaddikim “who see the face of the Shechinah every day”, “the eyes of the congregation”, who include them all.
- [Introduction.49]{.pnum} And in its plain sense: “in every gathering of ten” the Shechinah dwells, “and even Moses needed to pray with ten”.
- [Introduction.50]{.pnum} This surrounding light is revealed in the inwardness of Wisdom, “the nullification of being into nothing, as it is, as actual nothing”. Therefore “even in contemplation the nullification of being into nothing is grasped”, “which is not so in the love of ‘with all your soul’, which is the surrounding light of *Chayah* alone”.

## The Discourse with Its Explanation

:::: {.opening}
### 1. The verses, and the analogy of two fighters

[1.1]{.pnum} **An explanation of the foundation of prayer, which is called “a time of battle,” based on “He redeemed in peace”**: the heading of the discourse.
::::

[1.2]{.pnum} **(1) “He redeemed my soul in peace from the battle against me, for among the many they were with me.”** A verse in Psalms (55:19), which the whole discourse explains. It opens with another verse, which uses two expressions for the end of G‑d’s enemies: **It is written: “For behold, Your enemies, O G‑d, for behold, Your enemies shall perish; all the workers of iniquity shall be scattered”** (Psalms 92:10). The difference between “shall perish” and “shall be scattered” is explained below, in chapter 4.

[1.3]{.pnum} First, an analogy: **the matter is like two people who fight one another, each wishing to defeat the other and subdue him beneath him: of necessity they draw close together, press together, and their bodies cling to one another**, for one cannot throw an opponent without taking hold of him; **this is called wrestling**[Wrestling (*hit’abkut*) is combat by grappling, body against body, in which the victor is the one who throws the other to the ground. The sages also read the word *vaye’avek*, “and he wrestled,” as from *avak*, dust: “they raised dust up to the Throne of Glory” (Chullin 91a). The discourse uses both sides: wrestling draws the fighters close (here), and it soils the victor with the dust of the vanquished (below, chapter 4).]{.fn}, **as in “and a man wrestled with him”** (Genesis 32:25), **the angel of Esau, who fought with Jacob, for, as is known, they wrestled and drew near in complete closeness and clinging.**

[1.4]{.pnum} **And although all who fight are certainly two complete opposites, for otherwise they would not fight at all**, since if they were close by nature they would have nothing to fight over, **and between any two things opposite by nature there is certainly always a vast distance, and they can never come close together, just as fire and water can never join together**, **yet when they wish to fight to defeat each other, they must then join and draw close to wrestle with each other, in utter union and clinging to one another**.

[1.5]{.pnum} War thus does the opposite of the fighters’ nature: it brings the distant close. **But this closeness and clinging is not true in itself; on the contrary, they were utterly distant and separate from each other, for they are complete opposites; it is only for the sake of the war and the victory, as each wishes to defeat the other, that this clinging and joining, called wrestling, is forced on them, as explained above**; **but be that as it may, they have in any case come to a state of clinging and joining at this time, when they fight together.** Although the closeness is not true, during the war it exists in fact, and the whole discourse is built on this foundation.

:::: {.opening}
### 2. Wrestling in the soul, and its aim: inclusion

[2.1]{.pnum} **Now it is certainly understood by itself that just as this clinging is in the physical matter of their bodies, two bodies clinging together, so it is in the spiritual dimension of their souls: the two souls cling and wrestle with each other; indeed, the main part of this clinging and wrestling is in their souls specifically, for the soul is the main thing, and from it follows the clinging of their bodies, as is known**: the body fights only because the soul wants to win.
::::

[2.2]{.pnum} **Accordingly there is certainly a mingling of the light of the life of their souls when they wrestle together**, the life of one soul mixing with the life of the other, **that is, because each wishes to overcome the light and life of the other’s soul and subdue it to his own soul, that it be included in it and not be a being and thing in itself standing against his soul at all**, that it not stand against him as a separate existence, **and therefore they wrestle together with their bodies as well, each to throw the other and subdue him to himself, that is, that he be included in him specifically and never again be able to raise his head as a being and thing in itself.**

[2.3]{.pnum} The example from the elements: **And as fire and water fight together: when fire prevails over water, the fire turns the whole nature of the water to be included in the nature of fire, like the boiling of water by fire, when the fire prevails over it until it drives out all the moisture in the water and it is entirely consumed, and as the fire of G‑d licked up all the water that Elijah poured on the wood, etc.** (I Kings 18:38), **and likewise the reverse: when water prevails over fire, it turns the whole nature of fire to be entirely included in the nature of water, as water extinguishes fire, cooling it and driving out all the force of its nature until it is entirely consumed and included in the nature of water, as is known from the books of natural science on the details of how the four elements are included in one another**[The four elements: fire, air, water and earth. In the natural science of earlier generations every body is composed of them, and in each body one of them prevails and the others are included in it. The discourse takes from this an analogy for two kinds of inclusion: in one the vanquished is truly transformed into the nature of the victor; in the other it remains within, merely not revealed (below, chapter 7).]{.fn} **by way of war and victory, which comes specifically through closeness and clinging to one another.**

<!--cont-->**And the greater the clinging and closeness of two opposites, the more one can prevail and include the other in itself**: the greater the closeness, the more complete the victory, **for otherwise they would remain separate from each other, each in its own essence and nature, not included in the other at all.** **And this too is the reason why two people wrestle, in soul and body, when they fight: so that one can defeat the other and he be included in him.**

<!--cont-->**For otherwise each remains standing as he was, neither included in the other at all, but two opposing opposites as before (and so it is understood, at any rate, that the main intent of the war, in this wrestling and closeness, is their inclusion in one another, which is union, the opposite of the separation there was between them before; and for this reason they must come to wrestling, as explained above).** The aim of war is not to destroy the opponent but that he be included in the victor. So war, though it involves separation, is directed toward unity.

:::: {.opening}
### 3. The application: the two souls

[3.1]{.pnum} **And as we see in the wars of kings, that the decisive end of the victory comes when they draw close to one another, etc., as is known**, the decision coming when the armies meet face to face, **so from all this the application is understood in the war of the two souls, the divine soul and the animal soul**[The two souls: a Jew has two souls. The divine soul is “a part of G‑d above,” and its nature is to rise and cleave to its source. The animal soul is the vital soul that animates the body; from it come the desires and the natural traits, and its nature is to descend toward the physical (Tanya, chapters 1–2).]{.fn}, **as is known regarding “and one nation shall be stronger than the other nation”** (Genesis 25:23), **“when this one rises, that one falls,” etc.**, **which was the general war of Jacob with the angel of Esau, that is, the holy attributes against the attributes of the *kelipah*, as explained elsewhere on the verse “and a man wrestled with him,” etc.; and the wise will understand.** Jacob’s struggle with the angel of Esau is the root of the war within every person: the holy attributes against the attributes of the *kelipah*[*Kelipah* (husk): a name for the forces opposed to holiness. As a husk covers the fruit, so they cover and conceal the divine light, and draw their life from it. “The attributes of *kelipah*” are the traits of the animal soul when they are drawn after desire.]{.fn}.
::::

[3.2]{.pnum} **For it is known that the natures of these two souls are two completely opposed opposites, as it is written: “the spirit of man is the one that rises,” etc., “and the spirit of the beast goes down below,” etc.** (Ecclesiastes 3:21). **The one rises above, to the height of the heights, cleaving and binding itself to the living G‑d, the source from which it was hewn**, the source it was carved from, **for it is a part of G‑d, etc., as it is written: “For G‑d’s portion,” etc.** (Deuteronomy 32:9), **while the nature of the spirit of the beast is to descend and cleave far below, to the corporeality and materiality of physical lusts, like the conduct of an actual beast, as is known.** **This being so, they are certainly as distant and separate from each other as can be, and these two spirits cannot come close together.**

<!--cont-->**For they are utterly opposed to each other, like fire and water and the like**; **but this is specifically when they do not fight each other: then they are in a state of great separation and distance from each other.**

<!--cont-->**But when they fight together, they are pressed together and clinging to one another, in the manner of the war of two people in which one wants to defeat the other so that he be included in him, as explained above at length:** as long as the divine soul is occupied with itself alone, the animal soul is far from it. When it comes to transform it, the two press against each other, like the two fighters of the analogy.

:::: {.opening}
### 4. Prayer without war

[4.1]{.pnum} **(2) The matter is as stated in the Zohar: “the time of prayer is a time of battle,” etc.**[“The time of prayer is a time of battle”: a phrase of the Zohar, often cited in Chassidic works on the service of prayer. In prayer the divine soul seeks to refine and elevate the animal soul, and the animal soul fights against it.]{.fn} The discourse explains what this war means, and first points out that not every prayer is a war: **for in truth there are two levels in the service of prayer.**
::::

<!--cont-->**The first is not called battle and war at all: it is the service of the divine soul from its own side and nature alone, which is the nature of the spirit of man that rises, etc., as explained above**: the divine soul rises to its source because that is its nature, and does not turn to the animal soul at all. **And as is known regarding the prayer of Shabbat, which is only to delight in G‑d, etc.**, for on Shabbat the service of prayer is not the refining of evil but delight in G‑dliness, **which is the inclusion of the light of the divine soul in the source from which it was hewn, rising there like the nullification and inclusion of a candle before a torch**, like a candle whose light is nullified and absorbed in the light of a great torch, **like the souls in the Garden of Eden that delight in the radiance, etc.**, in the words of the sages: “the righteous sit with their crowns on their heads and delight in the radiance of the Shechinah” (Berachot 17a).

[4.2]{.pnum} **So too the spirit of the divine soul is aroused at the time of prayer to cleave to and be included in the unity of G‑d by the very essence of its nature, and it does not transform the nature of the spirit of the beast in the left chamber of the heart**[The animal soul dwells in the left chamber of the heart, the source of the desires; the divine soul dwells in the brain and extends to the right chamber of the heart (Tanya, chapter 9).]{.fn} **at all, so that it too would agree and wish to rise to G‑d, etc.**

<!--cont-->**Rather, the nature of the spirit of the beast remains in its essence, in a state of separation, etc., and only the light of the soul, from its own side, cleaves and rises higher and higher, as is known regarding giving up one’s soul at “One,” etc.**: in the Shema, at the word “One,” the soul gives itself to G‑d from its own nature, and as yet nothing changes in the animal soul. **That is, when there is no war at all between these two spirits, but each stands at its own level, separate from the other, and neither comes near the other at all.**

:::: {.opening}
### 5. A time of battle

[5.1]{.pnum} **But the second level in prayer, when it is called battle and war, as stated in the Zohar, is when the spirit of man prevails with flames of fiery longing for G‑d to transform also the spirit of the beast in the physical body, that it too will not desire with any foreign will and longing, but only G‑d alone, and will despise every foreign will and desire, and want nothing of the vanities of this world, only G‑d alone, as it is written: “Whom have I in heaven? And besides You I desire nothing,” etc.** (Psalms 73:25), **and as our sages said: “one heart for one’s Father in heaven,” etc., which is only one will, for G‑d alone, etc.** Here the soul is not content with its own ascent: it wants the animal soul too to want G‑d alone, so that there will not be two wills in the person.
::::

[5.2]{.pnum} **This cannot be except by way of war and victory, when the divine soul fights the animal soul and wishes to subdue it, that it be included in it and not be an opponent at all**, **which is to transform its nature entirely, so that it will not descend to cleave to the corporeality and materiality of the conduct of a beast, but will rise above in will and longing, like the spirit of man that rises, etc.** And in the terms of the analogy: **it is like one who prevails over the other and defeats him so that he is included in him and is no longer a separate thing in itself at all.**

<!--cont-->**And as explained above regarding the two opposites, fire and water, when one prevails over its opposite and transforms it to be like its own nature, etc.** **If so, this can certainly only be when these two souls draw close and cling, in the manner of those who fight together, which is called wrestling, as in “and a man wrestled with him”.**

<!--cont-->**And this is what the Zohar says, “the time of prayer is a time of battle”: the two souls fight each other, and each wishes to prevail over the other, to subdue it and include it in itself**, the divine soul to raise the animal soul, and the animal soul to bring the divine soul down, **and this is the root of the matter of stray thoughts in prayer, as is known**[Stray thoughts (*machshavot zarot*): thoughts of worldly matters and desires that arise in a person’s heart precisely during prayer and confuse him. Their reason is explained in chapter 3.]{.fn}.

:::: {.opening}
### 6. A true prayer, and what follows it

[6.1]{.pnum} Here is the answer to a question in the heart of everyone who prays: **for we see that prayer can be with intent, and yet immediately after prayer one goes and busies himself with the vanities of the world with all his heart and soul, as if he had not prayed at all**, **and it seems to a person that this prayer was not true at all, for had it been true, with all his heart and soul, it is said: “The lip of truth shall be established forever, but a lying tongue is only for a moment,” etc.** (Proverbs 12:19): what is true stands forever, and what is false lasts only a moment; and if the power of the prayer did not remain, it seems it was not true.
::::

[6.2]{.pnum} **But the truth is that this prayer is called “a lip of truth” on the side of the arousal of the divine soul, for the spirit of man truly rises by its nature and root, as explained above, and this service of the divine soul in itself “shall be established forever,” and never changes, as one says: “the soul You gave me is pure,” etc.**: the soul itself is always pure, and its service is true.

[6.3]{.pnum} **But that after prayer he again binds himself to the foreign wills of the vanities of the world comes from the arousal of the animal spirit, which remains in the body in its full strength and did not move at all from its place and nature during prayer and the service of the divine soul; for the animal soul is distant and separate and has no connection at all with the service of the divine soul.**

<!--cont-->**On the contrary, it flees far from this G‑dly light in the divine soul; and of this it is said, “but a lying tongue is only for a moment.”** The falsehood in the verse is not in the prayer itself, but in the arousal of the animal soul: **for even if the animal soul is sometimes aroused in prayer to rise as well in the will of G‑d, etc., this is only for a moment, and at once it changes and returns to its corruption, to be drawn below, like the nature of the spirit of the beast that goes down below, etc.**

[6.4]{.pnum} **But if the divine soul fights the animal soul and defeats it, subduing the other side, etc.**[*Itkafya sitra achra*: the subduing of “the other side,” the forces of evil, so that they are nullified before holiness. On the difference between subduing (*itkafya*) and transforming (*ithapcha*), see below, chapters 6–7.]{.fn}, **to subdue it utterly, until it is entirely included, so that it too, like the spirit of man, rises above, which is only one will, for G‑d alone, and no stranger with Him, etc., as explained above, then of it too it is said, “the lip of truth shall be established forever,” for it does not change, since they have been bound and included in the divine soul so that it can no longer separate into an essence of its own; as with complete tzaddikim, whose hearts are in their power, because the soul in the brain rules over the heart, etc.; and the wise will understand:** “The wicked are in the power of their hearts, but the righteous have their hearts in their power” (Bereishit Rabbah 34:10). A complete tzaddik need not fight his heart anew each time, for the mind rules over it.

:::: {.opening}
### 7. Why stray thoughts grow strong in prayer

[7.1]{.pnum} **(3) Now when the spirit of man fights the spirit of the beast, they must wrestle and draw close together, and this is the root reason why stray thoughts grow strong specifically at the time of prayer, more than at any other time.** Thoughts a person does not think all day rise before him precisely when he stands to pray, and this is **because the time of prayer is a time of battle, in which the divine soul fights to defeat the animal soul, and the main victory comes only through wrestling, through closeness specifically, as in the wars of two people, etc.**
::::

<!--cont-->**And so in prayer specifically: when the divine soul is aroused to transform the evil in the animal soul as well, it then draws close and wrestles with the animal soul, and the animal soul unites with and clings to the divine soul to confuse it with stray thoughts, for it is the very opposite of the spirit of man; and therefore it then specifically gathers all its strength to confuse it with all kinds of stray thoughts, evil thoughts such as would not arise outside the time of prayer.**

<!--cont-->**For in time of war specifically, it is the nature of the fighter to prevail with all his strength over the other, to defeat him and subdue him so that he be included in him, as explained above; and so the animal soul gathers all its strength to subdue the divine soul so that it will not rise above, but will descend and be drawn far below, to be like the nature of the animal soul that goes down below, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And this is exactly the strengthening of stray thoughts, etc.** Stray thoughts are therefore no sign that the prayer has failed. They are a sign that it touches the animal soul, and that the war has begun. **Which is not so when the divine soul does not wish to fight the animal soul, but its service is from its own essence alone, and not to transform the animal soul at all (as in the matter of the prayer of Shabbat, as explained above); then stray thoughts do not grow strong at all, as is known; and the wise will understand.**

:::: {.opening}
### 8. A thought is never lost

[8.1]{.pnum} **Now it is known that just as the soul itself is never lost or destroyed, so too none of its powers that extend from it are ever lost or destroyed; and accordingly even a single thought that a person thinks is never lost, for it is not a physical thing that ends and perishes, etc.** **And since thought is an extension of light and life from the essence of the soul, it must have eternal existence, just as the essence of the soul endures**: thought is the light of the soul itself, and what extends from something eternal is eternal like it.
::::

[8.2]{.pnum} **If so, when the soul leaves the prison of the body, all the powers that extend from it leave with it, and even a single thought that he thought leaves with it and is never lost; and accordingly, of all the stray thoughts, not for G‑d, that a person thought all the days of his fleeting life in this world, not one is missing when the soul leaves the body; and all the letters of speech also live and endure, as explained elsewhere (and the proof is from what is written: “He tells a man what his speech was,” even light conversation, etc.** (Amos 4:13; Chagigah 5b: “even the light conversation between a man and his wife is told to a person at the time of his death”), **but the breath of the letters of speech remains in the air, whereas the letters of thought become one with the soul, etc.).** Speech goes out of a person and remains outside him; thought remains within him and becomes one with his soul.

:::: {.opening}
### 9. No existence without a vessel: the destructive forces

[9.1]{.pnum} **Now it is also known that the soul has no existence without extension into a vessel, which is the body, just as light has no existence without wood and wick, etc.**[Light and vessel: every spiritual light needs a vessel in which to be clothed in order to exist and act, as the light of a candle depends on the wick and the oil. The soul is clothed in the body, and even in the Garden of Eden it is clothed in a spiritual garment. The discourse applies this rule to thoughts as well, which are the soul’s light.]{.fn} **Therefore we never find that the soul exists without a vessel: if the soul leaves this body, it immediately extends into another body, and also the souls that rise to the Garden of Eden are clothed and extended there specifically in a vessel, as is known and explained elsewhere.**
::::

[9.2]{.pnum} **If so, it is understood by itself that the thoughts too, which are the extension of its light, are like it in this: they cannot exist without extension into a vessel, since they are spiritual light and life, and no spiritual light and life has existence without being clothed and extended in a vessel, which is the body, etc.** From the two premises, that a thought is never lost and that it cannot exist without a vessel, the conclusion follows: **and this is why we find that a complete soul, animating a body, can come into being from many stray thoughts that gather together and become the light and life of a complete soul, like the living soul of the destructive forces made from people’s stray thoughts, and they are the ones that afflict and pursue a person**[Destructive forces and accusers (*mazikin*, *mekatregim*): spiritual forces that afflict a person and argue against him. According to the discourse they are created from the person’s own evil thoughts, so his afflictions come from within him and are not foreign to him.]{.fn} **(as explained elsewhere, that from a lofty soul its stray thoughts fall and become a complete soul below, clothed in a body, and this is the accuser that pursues and afflicts him, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And there are lofty souls from whose stray thoughts, their fallen leaves and leftovers, an abundant flow will come about below, as in the analogy of a leaf of a lofty tree from which fruit comes into being on a tree below it, etc.)**: even what falls from a high level, like a leaf falling from a tall tree, can be a rich flow for the level below it.

[9.3]{.pnum} **So it emerges that from the many stray thoughts of the spirit of the beast come many enemies and haters, called accusers and destructive forces, that pursue a person’s soul and become a strap for striking him, etc.**, a strap with which to whip him, **because they come clothed in bodies, until they are like separate enemies, though in truth they are his own thoughts, which he thought with the soul and spirit of the beast all his days, etc.** **Accordingly it is understood that when stray thoughts grow strong at the time of prayer, it is as if his enemies and haters were rising against him to harm him and throw him down, etc.; and the wise will understand:** this is the link to the verse “For behold, Your enemies,” explained next.

:::: {.opening}
### 10. The enemies of G‑d, and their nourishment at the time of prayer

[10.1]{.pnum} **(4) And this is “For behold, Your enemies, O G‑d,” etc. For it is known that the Name *Havayah* shines in every divine soul, etc., because “in the image of G‑d He made man” with two Names, *Havayah* and *Elokim*, as it is written: “And *Havayah Elokim* formed man,” etc.**[The Names *Havayah* and *Elokim*: two divine Names with which man was made, as it says, “And the L‑rd G‑d formed man” (Genesis 2:7). *Havayah* (the four-letter Name) denotes the divine light that is above nature; *Elokim* denotes the power that contracts it and clothes it in nature. *Havayah* shines in the divine soul.]{.fn} **And the enemies who oppose the Name *Havayah* that shines in the divine soul are the strengthening of stray thoughts in the animal soul, specifically at the time of prayer, when it gathers all its strength to fight the divine soul, as explained above.**
::::

<!--cont-->**For the time of prayer specifically is a time of battle, as explained above (and as in the analogy of fighters who wrestle together, etc., so the animal soul wrestles, takes hold and clings in tremendous closeness to the divine soul, to defeat it with stray thoughts, as explained above).** “Your enemies, O G‑d” are therefore the stray thoughts, the enemies of the Name *Havayah* in the soul.

[10.2]{.pnum} **And then specifically, the enemies of G‑d, which are the stray thoughts, receive added nourishment and life from the G‑dly light in the divine soul**, and this is something new: evil receives added life from the divine light itself. **For in truth no evil descends from above, as it says, “no evil shall dwell with You,” etc.** (Psalms 5:5), **and likewise “from the mouth of the Most High do not come forth the evils,” etc.** (Lamentations 3:38); **rather, evil draws its nourishment from the waste of the good, as is known from the root of the matter of the Tree of Knowledge of good and evil, etc.**[The Tree of Knowledge of good and evil: evil has no life of its own; it lives on the waste of the good, on what remains of the good where good and evil are mixed together. Therefore as long as evil is mixed with good, it has something to draw on.]{.fn}

[10.3]{.pnum} **And wherever there is much good, there the closeness and clinging of evil is found, to draw more nourishment, as our sages said: “whoever is greater than his fellow, his inclination is greater,” etc.** (Sukkah 52a), **and like robbers, night bandits, who risk their lives specifically where there is a great treasure, etc., as explained elsewhere.** **And this is the reason it is specifically at the time of prayer: because then the divine soul gathers all its strength to transform the animal soul as well, as explained above, then specifically the animal soul draws near with all its strength to cling to the divine soul with stray thoughts, to receive extra nourishment, because through this wrestling it comes into a greater mingling of good and evil, etc.**: in the struggle, good and evil are more mingled than at any other time, and in this mingling evil has something to draw on.

:::: {.opening}
### 11. The wrestler is soiled

[11.1]{.pnum} **For it is known that whoever wrestles with a filthy one becomes filthy too when he wrestles with him, etc.; for since he must wrestle with him in great clinging and closeness, even though he defeats him and throws him down, yet when they are locked together he is soiled and the filth of the filthy one clings to him, and afterward he needs washing and great scrubbing, etc.; and so it was when Jacob wrestled with the angel of Esau: although he defeated him, he was soiled by this dust, as our sages said, “they raised dust,” etc.** (Chullin 91a).
::::

[11.2]{.pnum} Even the victor comes out of the struggle soiled. **(And similarly in every refinement, the one who refines must soil his clothes when he puts them on to refine, by way of the filthy garments of what is being refined**[Refinement (*birur*): separating the good from the evil with which it is mixed, and raising the good to holiness. One who engages in refinement must go down to the place where good is mixed with evil, and therefore does not come out of it entirely clean.]{.fn}, **as is explained regarding the removal of the ashes, that he must put on lesser garments to take out the ashes, because he must be soiled by the ashes**, as it is said of the priest who takes out the ashes, “he shall take off his garments and put on other garments” (Leviticus 6:4), **and as our sages said, “the garments in which he cooked a pot for his master,” etc.** (Shabbat 114a: “the garments in which he cooked a pot for his master, let him not pour a cup for his master in them”).

[11.3]{.pnum} **And so above, in the refinements of the Name *Ban*, throughout the exile of the Shechinah among the nations, etc.**[The Name *Ban*: one of the fillings of the four-letter Name (numerically fifty-two), which is the level of *Malchut* (Kingship) and the Shechinah. The refinements are made at this level, and it is this level that went into exile with Israel among the nations, to refine the sparks within them.]{.fn}, **as our sages said, “they were exiled to Edom, the Shechinah is with them,” etc.** (Megillah 29a), **as it is written: “Who is this who comes from Edom, with garments stained… I have trodden the winepress alone… and their lifeblood is sprinkled on My garments, and I have stained all My raiment”** (Isaiah 63:1–3), **and as explained elsewhere at length, etc.; and the wise will understand)**: even the refinement above, in exile, involves soiling the garments.

[11.4]{.pnum} **And so it is when the divine soul wrestles with the animal soul in great closeness: although it wins and transforms all the evil of the stray thoughts, so that every foreign will, apart from G‑d alone, is uprooted, etc., still, while they wrestle, the divine soul is soiled with the filthy garments of the animal soul, which are the stray thoughts and the like, and it needs washing and great scrubbing.**

[11.5]{.pnum} **And this is what it says: “I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them?” etc.** (Song of Songs 5:3), **and as explained elsewhere on “when G‑d shall have washed away the filth,” etc.** (Isaiah 4:4): **let the mother come and wipe away her son’s filth**, as a mother cleans her child, **which is the divine soul, from the filthy garments of the animal soul when they wrestle together; and the wise will understand.** The divine soul itself is pure, but in the struggle it is soiled with the garments of the animal soul, the stray thoughts, and needs washing afterward.

### 12. The evil nearest to the good

[12.1]{.pnum} **And so it was with the mixed multitude that went up from Egypt with Israel, who were the rabble among them that lusted a lust, etc.** (Numbers 11:4), **and brought harm upon Israel; for any evil that is closer to, and clings more to, the good does more harm to the good, to defile it, etc.** The mixed multitude went up from Egypt with Israel, and precisely their closeness to them brought harm on Israel.
::::

[12.2]{.pnum} **And as explained elsewhere regarding the afterbirth of the fetus, that the greatest haters and enemies of Israel, the holy nation, are the idolaters who are closest to the good**: the evil nearest to the good, like the afterbirth attached to the fetus, does the most harm, **like the heretics, who know their Master, etc., of whom it is said, “I hate them with utmost hatred,” etc.** (Psalms 139:22), **and even converts from the nations are as hard for Israel as a sore, etc.; and the wise will understand.** (Yevamot 47b).

### 13. “Your enemies, O G‑d”: a break, and only for the moment

[13.1]{.pnum} **And this is “For behold, Your enemies, O G‑d,” etc., “shall perish,” etc.: “the enemies of G‑d” are those closest to the good in the totality of the souls of Israel, who cling to receive added nourishment and life, as when the man wrestled with Jacob, when in that embrace the angel of Esau received added nourishment from Jacob of holiness.** **And this is what it says: “Your enemies, O G‑d, shall perish,” with a *pasek* between them**[*Pasek*: a cantillation mark, a short line marking a pause in reading between two words. In the verse “For behold, Your enemies, O G‑d,” there is a *pasek* between “Your enemies” and G‑d’s Name, and the discourse learns from it that there is a complete separation between the enemies and the Name *Havayah*, although during the struggle they cling to it.]{.fn}, **because although the cause of the enemies’ added nourishment from holiness comes from the closeness and clinging of their wrestling, as explained above, nevertheless this wrestling is in essence not a true clinging and union in itself.**
::::

<!--cont-->**On the contrary, they come to oppose and divide, so that through it evil prevails over good in a state of separation below, as explained above.** **So it is understood that with that very divine light and life which they receive through the closeness and clinging of this wrestling, they grow strong to oppose G‑d, until they are called actual enemies of G‑d; and therefore there is a *pasek* between them, to show the break and complete separation that this clinging makes in *Havayah*, etc.** The life they take from holiness serves them to stand against it; the clinging itself is what breeds the separation.

[13.2]{.pnum} **But he said that “Your enemies, O G‑d,” who fight with Him, “shall perish,” etc., because all this nourishment and the strengthening of the light and flow to the externals**[Externals (*chitzonim*): a name for the forces of the *kelipah*, which are “outside” holiness and draw from it.]{.fn} **when they wrestle with the divine holiness is only for the moment, as the saying goes, “the hour smiles on them,” etc., and it is only the time of battle.** The enemies’ strength lasts only as long as the war.

### 14. After the victory: “shall perish”

[14.1]{.pnum} **But after the war is over, when the divine soul prevails over the animal soul and defeats it, so that it too gives up its soul and all its will for G‑d alone, as explained above, and as it is said of Jacob, “for you have striven with G‑d,” etc.** (Genesis 32:29), **“he acknowledged him and blessed him,” etc.**, the angel of Esau conceded the blessings to Jacob, **and so it is in the totality of the souls of Israel, as in detail in every divine spark in each and every Jew, who is called “Israel” for this reason, “for you have striven,” etc., as is known.** Every Jewish soul is called “Israel” for this victory.
::::

[14.2]{.pnum} **And this is in saying “Hear, O Israel,” etc., and from this comes “And you shall love,” etc., “with all your heart,” with both your inclinations, etc., specifically, as is known**[“With all your heart (*levavcha*)”: the doubled letter is read as two hearts: “with both your inclinations, the good inclination and the evil inclination” (Berachot 54a). The evil inclination too is to love G‑d.]{.fn}, **that is, that the evil too is transformed into good, or utterly subdued, as with a complete tzaddik, whose heart is in his power.**

<!--cont-->**For the mind rules over the heart, which is called subduing (*itkafya*), as explained above**, **then, on the contrary, every root of divine life is cut off and stops, no longer drawn to the externals, even in the restricted measure they had before**: not only do they lose the addition they took in the war, but even their fixed portion of life is cut off.

[14.3]{.pnum} **And this is what it says, “for behold, Your enemies shall perish,” because even the original root of their nourishment is cut off from them, and then they perish of themselves, for evil has no existence and no hope except from the good, as explained above; and this is “and for the slanderers let there be no hope,” etc.**, in the words of the prayer, **and as explained elsewhere on “he swallowed wealth and shall vomit it up”** (Job 20:15), **and likewise “I will bring out of his mouth what he swallowed”** (Jeremiah 51:44): **that all the light and life it swallowed from holiness through the war and the wrestling it will vomit up.**

<!--cont-->**For all the good will be drawn out of it, even what it had from the very creation, according to the principle of the measuring line**[The measuring line (*kav hamidah*): the restricted life given to the *kelipot* from the beginning of creation, in measure and allotment, so that there may be free choice. After the victory, even this life is taken from them.]{.fn}, **for this is the way of war: when one defeats an enemy and foe, he becomes a servant even with what is his own, as in “and you shall eat the spoil of your enemies,” etc.** (Deuteronomy 20:14).

[14.4]{.pnum} **(And we find an example of this in Abraham our father, peace be upon him, with the four kings, as it is written: “and he divided himself against them by night, and smote them,” etc.** (Genesis 14:15): **this dividing is the matter of the *pasek* in “Your enemies, O G‑d,” explained above, that is, that the letter *hei* of *lailah*, night, was divided off, so that it became like the night of watching, etc., because the lower *hei*, which is *Malchut*, gives life to the externals as well**[The lower *hei*: the final *hei* of the four-letter Name, the level of *Malchut*, from which life flows to the worlds below, and, in a contracted way, to the externals as well. *Lailah*, night (spelled with a *hei*), is the level of *Malchut*; when the *hei* is divided off from it, the externals have no more life.]{.fn}, **as it is written, “and His kingship rules over all”** (Psalms 103:19), **and the night was divided off from them so that it would no longer give life to the externals, because the Patriarchs are the chariot**, entirely nullified to the supreme will, as a chariot is nullified to its rider, **and Abraham, peace be upon him, separated from the animal soul in his body all waste and evil, until they had no side of closeness and clinging by which to take hold of and cling to the lower *hei* at all, as after the completion of the divine soul’s victory over the animal soul, as explained above.**

<!--cont-->**And then of itself the externals have no life at all, as it says regarding “their protection is removed from them”** (Numbers 14:9), **that they became like beasts that fear man; therefore “one shall pursue a thousand,” etc.** (Deuteronomy 32:30), **and then “he smote them”; and this is what it says, “he makes his sword as dust”** (Isaiah 41:2): **he threw dust and it became swords, etc.; and the wise will understand.)** So Abraham fought the four kings: not the strength of his army decided it, but that nothing was left in him that the externals could take hold of.

### 15. “Shall perish” and “shall be scattered”

[15.1]{.pnum} **Now as to what it says, “shall perish,” and afterward, “all the workers of iniquity shall be scattered,” etc., the matter is that, as explained above, a complete soul in a body can come into being from many stray thoughts, etc., and this is called “your enemies who pursued you,” etc.** (Deuteronomy 30:7). **But after the victory of the divine soul over the animal soul, when all the earlier stray thoughts are also transformed to be for G‑d alone, with no stranger with Him, etc., then all the stray thoughts the soul thought before will no longer extend to come into bodies.**
::::

<!--cont-->**But will perish entirely, like waste that is lost and cast out, as in the destruction of the wicked, since all the good has been drawn out of them through repentance from the depth of the heart, uprooting every foreign will, etc.** Repentance draws out of the old thoughts the good that was in them, and what remains is waste alone: **if so, what life remains in them is only utter waste, which has no repair other than its destruction: that is its repair, as it says, “when the wicked perish there is song”** (Proverbs 11:10), **that is their song and repair, as in the analogy of the leech that sucks out the waste blood and falls and dies.**

<!--cont-->**And this is what it says, “evil shall slay the wicked”** (Psalms 34:22), **and as our sages said, in the time to come He will slaughter the evil inclination, etc.** (Sukkah 52a). **And this is what it says, “for behold, Your enemies, O G‑d,” which cannot be refined, “shall perish” entirely, etc.**: “shall perish” is said of the thoughts that have not yet been clothed in bodies.

[15.2]{.pnum} **And as to what it says, “shall be scattered,” etc., these are the stray thoughts of every kind of evil that have already extended and descended to be clothed in bodies, and have become destructive forces and accusers to afflict and pursue a person, which are called “the plagues of the children of men,” whether spiritual or physical, as explained above; of these he said that even those bodies “shall be scattered”: although they are already clothed in bodies, they will do no evil at all, because they will be divided and greatly scattered, as our sages said, “the scattering of the wicked is good for them,” etc.** (Sanhedrin 71b); **for the gathering of all the stray thoughts becomes a multitude standing in opposition to them, as explained above, but when they are scattered and divided, none of them has any strength or force to stand in opposition.**

<!--cont-->**On the contrary, each is subdued and utterly nullified in its smallness, etc.** The strength of the destructive forces was in their gathering; when they are scattered, each alone is nullified. **And this is what it says, “Arise, O G‑d, and let Your enemies be scattered,” etc.** (Numbers 10:35), **because “when this one rises, that one falls,” etc., so that when there is a complete structure, a whole stature, in the Congregation of Israel, in wisdom, understanding and knowledge and in the attributes, etc., then in the opposite side there is scattering and division, etc.**[“This opposite that”: for every power in holiness there is a corresponding power in the *kelipah*, and when one grows strong the other weakens. A “whole stature” is when all the powers of the soul, the intellect and the attributes, are built up in holiness.]{.fn}

[15.3]{.pnum} **And this is what it says, “shall perish,” of the stray thoughts that have not come into bodies, and “shall be scattered,” even those that have come into bodies, as in every physical war, in which many are killed and perish entirely, and many are dispersed and scattered, as it is written, “let them be scattered and let them flee,” etc.** **And this is specifically now, when prayer is called battle; but of the time to come it is written: “they shall not harm nor destroy,” etc., “the lion shall eat straw like the ox, and the wolf shall dwell with the lamb, and the cow and the bear,” etc.** (Isaiah 11:6–9), **because of the nullification that will be upon all creatures when the glory of *Havayah* is revealed, etc., which is after all the refinements of now, as it is written, “for then I will turn,” etc., “a pure language”** (Zephaniah 3:9), **so that there will be no need for war at all, but all will be in true nullification, etc.**

<!--cont-->**(as will be explained shortly on “He redeemed my soul in peace,” etc.):** here the heart of the discourse is hinted: beyond war and victory there is a level where there is no need for war at all.

### 16. War belongs only to the inner light

[16.1]{.pnum} **(5) Now the whole matter of the war described above, of the divine soul with the animal soul, applies only to the part of the divine soul that is clothed in the body, which is its *Nefesh, Ruach* and *Neshamah*, which come as inner light, light in a vessel, in limitation, etc., as is known**[*Nefesh, Ruach, Neshamah, Chayah, Yechidah*: five levels of the soul, from lowest to highest. <!--cont-->**But the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* of the divine soul, which are entirely above the limitation of light in the vessel of the body, for it is only surrounding light, and it is the great love of “with all your might,” as is known: there one cannot speak of war with the animal soul at all, etc.; and the wise will understand.** Here lies the foundation for all that follows: there is war only where the divine soul and the animal soul meet, in the vessels, in the mind and the heart. What is above the vessels never meets the animal soul, and there is no war there.

### 17. The analogy: the life of the soul in the body

[17.1]{.pnum} **The explanation of the matter: it is known and understood, by way of analogy, from the light and life of the soul in the body in general, that there are two levels in it, as is known. [17.2]{.pnum} **But the surrounding light of the soul is the part of the soul that is its essence, which is the core and the source from which the extension of the soul’s light and life into the limbs of the body comes and is drawn, to each one according to its temperament, etc.**, **and accordingly it is understood that this source is called surrounding, for it encircles and surrounds all the light and life that comes as inner light into the vessels of the body from head to foot, and includes them all together, because in itself it is above the division of head and foot, since it is above being clothed in a vessel.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore of itself it shines as surrounding and encircling the whole body, head and foot, in exactly one equality, etc., with no difference of level at all between the brain in the head and the toenail and the like, as explained elsewhere; and the wise will understand.** “Surrounding” is not a place outside the person. It is the essence of the soul, the source of all its life, and because it is clothed in no particular limb it is equal in all of them.

[17.3]{.pnum} **So too is understood the difference between the surrounding light of the divine soul, called *Chayah* and *Yechidah*, and the inner light, called *Nefesh, Ruach, Neshamah*, as explained above: its powers of mind, wisdom, understanding and knowledge, are a grasping and comprehension in inner light, grasped in the vessel of the brain, and are called *Neshamah*, as is known that the *Neshamah* dwells in the brain and the *Ruach* in the heart, etc.**[Wisdom, Understanding, Knowledge (*Chabad*) and Kindness, Might, Beauty (*Chagat*): Wisdom, Understanding and Knowledge are the powers of the intellect, “the mind”; Kindness, Might and Beauty head the attributes, which are in the heart. <!--cont-->**And likewise the *Ruach* in the heart, of which it says, “G‑d weighs the spirits”** (Proverbs 16:2), for the attributes in the heart are measured, **and as explained elsewhere; and all this is inner light limited in a vessel, like the analogy of the light and life of the soul in the limbs of the body, in each one according to its temperament, as explained above**; **but the surrounding light of the divine soul is the essence of the divine soul, which is above extension and being clothed in the vessels of the mind, wisdom, understanding and knowledge, and of the heart, kindness, might and beauty, etc.**

<!--cont-->**Rather, it is the core and source from which comes the extension of the lights of *Nefesh, Ruach, Neshamah* into the vessels of mind and heart, etc.; therefore its light surrounds and encircles them equally, exactly, head and foot, with no difference of level at all, as explained above in the analogy of the surrounding light of the soul over the light and life of the physical limbs, etc.; and the wise will understand:** so within the divine soul itself there is what enters the intellect and the attributes and varies with their measure, and there is what is above them, always equal.

### 18. The *Yechidah*, and the two surrounding lights

[18.1]{.pnum} **(6) And this too is the difference between the love of “with all your soul,” which is inner light, *Nefesh, Ruach, Neshamah*, in mind and heart, etc., coming in great limitation, only according to the limitation of the vessels of mind and heart, etc., and the great love of “with all your might,” which is surrounding light, not limited in the vessel of the brain, etc.** The love in the mind and heart grows and shrinks with what a person grasps and feels; “with all your might” has no measure.

[18.2]{.pnum} **Of this our sages said: “in every gathering of ten, the Shechinah dwells,” etc.** (Sanhedrin 39a), **because in the *Yechidah* of the divine soul actual G‑dliness dwells, and the externals receive no nourishment there at all, as it says, “my only one from the hand of the dog,” etc.** (Psalms 22:21): the *Yechidah* is saved from every hold of the externals. **And this is what it says: “Even when I walk in the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil. [18.3]{.pnum} **Now there are two surrounding lights, *Chayah* and *Yechidah*, etc., as is known, and they are two kinds of will. The first is a will that shines and extends into the vessels of mind and heart, etc., for although its root is in the surrounding light, it nevertheless shines and comes into the mind and heart in a particular way, etc.; but the second will is called simple will, entirely above shining and extending into the mind and heart, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And this is the difference between the two surrounding lights, *Chayah* and *Yechidah*, which are called the general surrounding light and the particular surrounding light, as is written in *Etz Chaim*.** The *Chayah* is a surrounding light, yet it shines in the mind and heart, in a will a person feels; the *Yechidah* is a simple will that does not shine in them at all[Will (*ratzon*): in Chassidic teaching the will is above the intellect and the attributes and moves them. ### 19. A small will before a great one: subdued

[19.1]{.pnum} **The explanation of the matter: one must first consider what we see, that when a person has some will and longing for something, it can be entirely nullified and set aside before a will and longing far greater and more wonderful than it, when it is the opposite of this longing and will**, **and although, because of the strength of that wonderful longing, the first longing is subdued and entirely nullified, as if it had never been, nevertheless this is not a complete nullification, transforming its essence and nature, for its nature and essence have not been transformed at all.**
::::

<!--cont-->**On the contrary, it is there in its full strength and essence, and nothing of it has been nullified; only it does not shine openly in the mind and heart at all, because of the strength of the greater will and longing in the mind and heart at that time**, **but as soon as he removes from his mind and heart the light and will of the greater and more wonderful longing, this longing will return to its strength and nature, fully revealed, as it is, without the slightest nullification.** **So it emerges that its nullification and setting aside is not a true nullification, only a setting aside and nullification for the moment; and even this is not called a nullification of its essence at all, only the absence of its revelation, etc.**: the will was not nullified; it was only hidden.

[19.2]{.pnum} **Accordingly it is understood that even in war and victory, although this will defeats the will and longing that preceded it, because of its great strength over it, etc., nevertheless this is not a complete and true nullification; for though it nullifies it for the moment, it is not a nullification of its essence at all, only a subduing, so that it is entirely unrevealed, as if it did not exist at all, but in truth it is there in its full strength, as explained above.**

<!--cont-->**And this is called subdued and not transformed (*itkafya* and not *ithapcha*), etc.**[*Itkafya* and *ithapcha*: *itkafya* is the subduing of evil, which then does not act although its nature has not changed. *Ithapcha* is the transforming of the evil itself into good, “darkness into light.” The discourse distinguishes the two, and explains below (chapter 7) that even *ithapcha* achieved through war is not nullification in essence.]{.fn} An example from life: **as when a person desires and wants, with a lust for money, to earn great wealth.**

<!--cont-->**But because of it he will lose his own life or the life of the members of his household, who are dearer to him than any wealth, etc., or the like; or when some greatness and honour comes his way, to be a king or a minister, if he loses the profit or spends great wealth, and the like: then certainly all the wealth is as nothing compared with the greatness and honour he will attain, and he will set aside this will and longing for money for the will and longing far greater and more wonderful than it, which is the great honour and greatness, as is known.**

<!--cont-->**Yet the will for money has not been entirely nullified, for he certainly feels pain at the loss of money; only because of the strength of the will for honour and greatness, this will for money is nullified for the moment and does not shine openly at all, but it is in its nature and essence, only subdued and nullified in that its revelation does not extend, etc., as explained above; and the wise will understand.** The proof that the will for money was not nullified: he feels pain at the loss.

### 20. A war that subdues, and a war that transforms

[20.1]{.pnum} **And all this is because the whole coming into being of this will comes only as a revelation of inner light from the soul into the mind and heart, etc., which comes in limitation according to the vessel alone, etc.; therefore, when the revelation of another will greater than it comes, the light of the first will must withdraw from the mind and heart because of the strength of the revelation of the light of this will, etc.**: in the mind and heart there is limited room, and two wills do not shine there together; the greater drives the lesser out of revelation.
::::

[20.2]{.pnum} **And this is called war and victory, in which one defeats and subdues the other so that it withdraws and is nullified for the moment from its position, while he stands in his own, etc., like the analogy of two people who fight: when one defeats the other and throws him down, he stands in his place and position, etc.; but this is not a complete nullification of the other’s essence, only a subduing, so that he will not extend to raise his head; and the wise will understand.**

[20.3]{.pnum} **But the war and victory of two opposite elements, like fire and water, is not a matter of subduing alone, but a complete transformation of essence; for when fire prevails over water, it turns the nature of the water to be like itself, the very nature of fire, and likewise the reverse when water prevails over fire, etc., as is known from the composition of the opposite elements, that each one, when transformed and included in its opposite, adds to the nature of the other as if it were an essential part of it and not a compound, etc.; and this is the essence of true inclusion, as is known.** Here the vanquished is not merely hidden: it becomes part of the victor and adds to its strength.

### 21. Forbearance, and true peace

[21.1]{.pnum} **But when one merely bears its opposite, by way of forbearance and submission, and not nullification of its essence, this is not called inclusion, as it is said that Michael and Gabriel, the prince of fire and the prince of water, do not extinguish one another, etc.**, as the sages said of “He makes peace in His heights” (Job 25:2), **for it says only that they do not extinguish each other, being opposed to each other, but that each bears its opposite, not that the essence of each is nullified to be included in its opposite; like two opposing servants or ministers standing before the king, who tolerate each other because of the nullification upon them, from awe of the king, but in their very essence are truly two opposites, etc.**
::::

<!--cont-->**And this is what it says, “He makes peace,” etc., as explained elsewhere.** The peace between Michael and Gabriel is not inclusion; each remains as it is, only awe of the king does not let them fight.

[21.2]{.pnum} **But a war that transforms the essence, as explained above, is far higher than such peace; and true peace, in true inclusion, comes in two ways: one, through war that transforms the essence, as the wars of Joshua and David; or without war, as in the days of Solomon and the days of Moshiach, as explained elsewhere; and the wise will understand.** So there are three levels: the peace of forbearance, the lowest; war that transforms the essence; and true peace, which comes either through war or without it. From here it will finally become clear what “redeemed in peace” is.

[21.3]{.pnum} **So it emerges that there are two kinds of war. The first only subdues, *itkafya*, so that it does not extend, but is not a nullification of the essence, which is called true inclusion, as explained above; and the second is a war in which one prevails to transform and nullify the essence, as explained above, until it is included in him and becomes as an essential part of him, etc., which is called true inclusion, as explained above.** **Now both these kinds of war are only in the inner light, that is, as with two wills that are limited in the mind and heart as inner light, when one prevails over its opponent to subdue and nullify it, even with nullification of the essence, which is called inclusion, as explained above**, **and this applies also to the surrounding light called *Chayah*, which shines in the mind and heart.**

<!--cont-->**For although in itself it is a surrounding light, above the limitation of the vessels of the mind and heart, as is known that *Nefesh, Ruach, Neshamah* are clothed in the mind and heart, etc., but *Chayah* and *Yechidah* are not limited in vessels at all, as is written in *Etz Chaim* and as explained above; nevertheless, since it shines and is clothed in the vessels of the mind and heart, etc., this war is possible in it too.**

<!--cont-->**Which is not so of the *Yechidah*, as will be explained, with G‑d’s help; and the wise will understand:** the *Chayah*, though a surrounding light, shines in the mind and heart, and therefore war applies to it too; only the *Yechidah*, which does not shine in them, stays outside the war.

### 22. With all your heart, with all your soul, with all your might

[22.1]{.pnum} **(7) So too is understood with the two kinds of love, “with all your heart” and “with all your soul,” of which our sages said “with both your inclinations,” which is to subdue or to transform, with nullification and inclusion of all the foreign wills, so that the will is only for G‑d alone, as it is written, “Whom have I in heaven,” etc.; and this is through “with all your soul,” the surrounding light of *Chayah*, which strengthens the light of the divine will in the divine soul to subdue all the wills of the animal soul entirely, by way of war and victory, transforming the essence, as explained above**: “with all your heart” is the service of both inclinations, and it is done by the power of “with all your soul,” the surrounding light of the *Chayah*, which makes the divine will prevail over all the other wills.

[22.2]{.pnum} **And this is what we see, that the essence and nature of all the evil attributes can be transformed to be for G‑d alone, with no stranger with Him, only through the war of the divine soul, when it prevails over the animal soul at the time of prayer, which is then the time of battle, as explained above; and the wise will understand.** **But the great love of “with all your might” is the surrounding light of the *Yechidah*, which is entirely above limitation in the mind and heart, etc., that is, it never shines in the mind and heart, not even as a surrounding light.**

<!--cont-->**And it is called “your might,” without limit in its essence, and not merely without limit relative to the vessel, as the surrounding light of the *Chayah* explained above; there war is not possible at all; and this is the true self-sacrifice, that comes after the self-sacrifice of “with all your soul,” etc.; and the wise will understand.** The *Chayah* is without limit only relative to the vessels, being greater than they are; the *Yechidah* is without limit in itself.

### 23. The minority nullified in the majority

[23.1]{.pnum} **The explanation of the matter: it is known that there are two kinds of self-sacrifice. <!--cont-->**On the contrary, it has been transformed into another essence, which is to despise evil and choose good, which is against its former nature and essence, etc., as explained above.** This is a high level: the animal soul itself despises evil. <!--cont-->**And although it is by its nature opposed to the divine will, nevertheless its nature is transformed, like the transforming of a little bitter water in much sweet water, where the bitter taste is transformed into sweetness**, **or even when the foreign will is not small and the divine will great and abundant beyond it, but both are equal in quantity, etc.; but because of the strength of the will and longing for G‑d, when it is in the strength and depth of the heart, then even the foreign wills, though more numerous, are nullified in their essence and all transformed to G‑d, as the bitter is transformed into sweet even when the bitter is more, if the little sweet is very sweet, as with very sweet honey, in which the bitterness of radish is transformed into sweetness, also because of the strength of the sweetness of the honey, which prevails over it, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And an example of this is what is written, “and he cast in a tree, and the waters were sweetened”: although the tree was small and the waters of Marah very many, nevertheless they were sweetened.** (Exodus 15:25). ### 24. It returns and reawakens

[24.1]{.pnum} **But from all this it is understood that this nullification is not called nullification in essence, from its own side alone, but only because of the strength of the opposing power over it, until it transforms it, etc.; if so, it is only at this time, when the greater power prevails over it, but as soon as the greater light and power withdraws and goes away, the light and power of the former opposing will returns and reawakens, in its exact former strength and essence, etc.** And the proof from nature: **the proof of this is that we find in every inclusion of a thing and its opposite, as with bitter and sweet, or the inclusion of two elements, fire and water, and the like: when all the light, power and life of the great power that prevailed over the weaker and smaller is separated and drawn out, until it is entirely gone, then that small and weak power returns and reawakens, “let the weak say, I am strong,” etc.** (Joel 4:10), **and it is found in its existence exactly as it was at first, without any change of essence at all.**
::::

<!--cont-->**For if all the sweetness of the honey and the like is drawn out and separated, the bitterness of the radish will remain exactly as it was at first; and likewise the sweet water that sweetened the bitter: when all the parts of the sweet water are drawn out and separated, the little bitter water will be found as it was, etc.; and likewise in the inclusion of fire and water: when all the parts of fire are drawn out of wood, which is composed of the two elements, fire and water, then although, when the element of fire prevailed in it, all the parts of the element of water had already been burned and turned to fire, nevertheless when all the parts of fire are entirely removed, all the parts of water will be found in it, whole, as is known.** **And so it is understood with the nullification and inclusion of the foreign will when the divine will prevails over it: this is only at that time.**

<!--cont-->**But as soon as the light and power of the divine will withdraws, the power of the foreign will returns and reawakens as it was at first, etc.** This explains what a person sees every day: after a prayer in which the heart was transformed, the old wills return.

### 25. The surrender of the will

[25.1]{.pnum} **Nevertheless it is called actual self-sacrifice, since at the time the light of the divine will of the divine soul prevailed, all the power of the foreign will was nullified, and it too was transformed to despise evil and choose good, etc.**, and at that time it is true self-sacrifice; **but this is not self-sacrifice in its essence and being; it is only what is called the surrender of the will, in which the foreign will drawn from the soul is nullified and handed over to G‑d.**
::::

<!--cont-->**And therefore it is called by the word “handing over,” like a thing handed over from one domain to another, which is changed from its first essence for the time it is handed over to another domain; for while it is handed over to another domain, it becomes there like one of its essential parts; but there can be a change of domain, so that it leaves this domain for the first one.** A thing handed from hand to hand belongs to its new owners, but it may return.

[25.2]{.pnum} **And so, by way of example, with the surrender of the will: the foreign will left the public domain, the world of separation, and handed itself over to the private domain, the world of unity, etc.**[The private domain and the public domain (*reshut hayachid*, *reshut harabim*): two concepts from the laws of Shabbat, which Chassidus reads as two worlds: the “public domain” is the world in which things appear separate and many, and the “private domain” is the world of unity, in which all is nullified to the One of the world.]{.fn} **And although at the time it is handed over to the world of unity it is a complete nullification, the nullification of its first essence, as explained above, there can still be a change of domain, that is, when the divine will to whose domain it was handed over withdraws, it returns and enters the first domain, the public domain, the world of separation, etc., as the natural will of the animal soul, as it was before.**

<!--cont-->**For any handing over is not an essential nullification, only a handing over, which is for the moment, because of the domain that rules and prevails over it at that time alone, etc.; and the wise will understand:**

### 26. The surrender of the soul itself

[26.1]{.pnum} **(8) Now there is another self-sacrifice, far higher than the self-sacrifice explained above, in which the intent is not the surrender of the will alone, which is the handing over of the will to the domain of the will that prevails over it, as explained above; rather the intent in it is the surrender of the soul itself specifically, and not the surrender of the will alone.** **And although it is explained elsewhere that will is called “soul,” as in “if it is your will (*nafshechem*)”** (Genesis 23:8), **“my soul (*nafshi*) would not be,” etc.** (Jeremiah 15:1), in both places “soul” means will, **this is no comparison at all to the essence of the soul itself, from which every will and longing is drawn, as is known, etc.**
::::

[26.2]{.pnum} **Being so, the matter of a war and victory of two wills, in which one will prevails over its opposite until it transforms it, etc., is not possible here; since the soul itself is the source of all will and longing, as is known, it is understood that relative to the very essence of the soul a great will and a small will are equal, since the soul is entirely without will, and only from it is drawn the source of every will.**

<!--cont-->**So there is no difference at all between a great will and a small will.** **Not that the great will is a large part of the soul and the small will a small part of the soul, so that the great would have an advantage over the small, etc., since will is not part of the soul at all, for its essence is entirely without will, etc.**: all the wills, great and small, are only a radiance from the soul, and relative to the soul itself they are all equal.

[26.3]{.pnum} **If so, the level and degree of self-sacrifice in the soul’s own essence and being is understood, which is far above the surrender of the will alone; and the matter is that this is nullification in essence, from its own side and essence, when it itself is transformed, and not because of another, opposing power prevailing over it at all; only from its own essence and being, every aspect of the nature and essence of itself has been entirely and completely nullified, with nullification in essence, in which a cause of war and victory is not possible at all, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And this is the love of “with all your might,” which is the true self-sacrifice, that comes after the self-sacrifice of “with all your soul,” as explained above.** The difference: in the surrender of the will, power prevails over power; in the surrender of the soul itself there are not two powers: the soul itself is nullified.

### 27. Pleasure and pain

[27.1]{.pnum} To understand the matter fully, the discourse first lays a foundation: **to understand the root of this matter to its end, one must first set out a matter called equanimity, as is known**[Equanimity (*hishtavut*): evenness of soul, a trait in which the good and bad things of this world are equal in a person’s eyes, so that he neither rejoices at receiving them nor grieves at losing them. The discourse explains it through pleasure and pain, and from it arrives at the nullification of the *Yechidah*.]{.fn}.
::::

[27.2]{.pnum} **The matter is that we see that the core and source that includes everything in the world is two things, pleasure and pain, and this includes everything after which the soul is drawn, as is known; for the soul is drawn to anything only because of the pleasure in that thing, and that pleasure is what causes the will and desire with which it wants and desires it; without the pleasure it had in it, it would not want that thing at all, as is known**: pleasure comes before will and causes it.

[27.3]{.pnum} **For this reason we see that when something is done to a person against his will, he is moved with great pain in his soul, and this is called affliction of the soul; and at first glance this is a great wonder: where does this feeling of pain come from, so strong that it pains him to the point of diminishing the light and life in the essence of the soul with a complete diminution, which is called a crushed spirit or a broken spirit, as is known? Is not the soul spiritual, and how can breaking apply to it, etc.? And this diminution comes only from what is done against his will, while will itself is not of the essence of the soul, only a mere radiance, as is known.** The question: if will is only a radiance of the soul, how does a blow to the will break the soul itself? **Rather one must say that the light of pleasure shines and comes into the essence of the soul.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore pain, which is the opposite of pleasure and will, makes a diminution of light, as is known that *nega* (affliction) has the letters of *oneg* (pleasure), etc.**: pleasure touches the essence of the soul, so its opposite, pain, touches it too. *Nega* and *oneg* are the same letters in a different order, and affliction is the opposite of pleasure.

[27.4]{.pnum} **Now we see many levels, one above the other, in the diminution of light through pain: there are pains and afflictions that do not touch the soul so much, so that the pain can very easily be set aside, like the worry and pain of which it is said, “worry in a man’s heart, let him speak of it”** (Proverbs 12:25), **and through speech the pain and worry go out; or through musical instruments and laughter, or some other cause, like a stroll and other pleasures and the like.**

<!--cont-->**And there are pains and afflictions that touch the soul greatly, until he cannot set them aside or take his mind and heart off them with anything in the world; and all this is only because the root of pain and affliction is merely the opposite of pleasure and will, as explained above; therefore the abundance or scarcity of the pain depends on the abundance or scarcity of the pleasure and will; for if the pleasure and will are very great, until the inwardness of his soul delights and takes joy in it, then when something is done that strongly opposes that pleasure and will, the pain and affliction from it will be very great, making a very great diminution.**

<!--cont-->**For exactly according to the abundance of the pleasure, so will the pain be in the absence of the light of the pleasure, in measure and amount; the pain adds nothing to the pleasure and takes nothing from it, but they are of one measure and amount, since pain comes only from the diminution and absence of pleasure, etc.** This rule is the key to all that follows: the pain equals exactly the pleasure that was.

[27.5]{.pnum} **For example, the pain at the loss of great wealth will be great according to the great pleasure in that wealth, and at a small loss of it the pain will be small according to the small pleasure in it, etc.; and likewise with other, greater pleasures, like the pleasure of honour and greatness: so will the pain be great at the absence and fall of honour, etc.; and likewise with the pleasure of desire, according to the greatness of the pleasure, so will the pain be great at its absence, as in the story of one who set his heart on a woman, etc.** (Sanhedrin 75a), **and similarly, “once, when a lion and lions were standing,” etc.**, **and likewise there is a very restricted pleasure and will.**

<!--cont-->**So that the opposite done against the will is not called affliction so much, only a restricted and small pain, according to the smallness and restriction of the pleasure and will, etc.** **But although will and pleasure are not the actual essence of the soul, only a mere radiance from it, etc., nevertheless pain makes a diminution of light in the essence of the soul, because just as pleasure reaches the essence of the soul, so pain, at the absence of pleasure, reaches the essence of the soul, until it is called a broken and crushed spirit and the like; and the wise will understand (as explained elsewhere, that the soul itself takes delight in this particular pleasure, etc., as is known regarding the three meals of Shabbat, etc.; and the wise will understand).**

### 28. Equanimity

[28.1]{.pnum} **But we see that a person can have equanimity, that is, even in a thing the soul covets, like all the pleasures of this world, money and honour and the other desires and lusts of this world, he has no wish for them at all; on the contrary, he despises them utterly, as if his soul hated them in a natural and essential way; and this is an exceptional level and degree, with none above it, and is called the shedding of physicality, as with complete tzaddikim, whose souls are free and stripped of all will and longing for the physicality of this world; although this is against the nature of the animal soul, its first nature has been entirely nullified, and it has become as another nature, to despise the pleasures of this world entirely.** How does one know whether a person has truly reached this? **And the truth of this matter can be clarified to the end only from its opposite specifically, which is pain, the opposite of pleasure, which comes from it in the very same measure and amount, as explained above.**
::::

<!--cont-->**That is, if, when something in the matters of this world is done to him against his will, he is moved with pain and affliction, then he necessarily has some will and pleasure in this thing, according to which he feels pain at the absence of the will and pleasure, as explained above; if so, the physical nature has not been transformed and completely nullified in this man, and he is not yet called stripped of physicality.** The test is pain: what pains a person when it is taken from him is what he still takes pleasure in.

[28.2]{.pnum} **For example, when he suffers a loss of money or a fall in honour and the like, and is moved with pain in his soul, he necessarily has will and pleasure in money and honour and the like, and in the other desires and lusts of this world, etc.; and according to the pain, whether great or small, so must be the measure of the pleasure and will he has in money and honour and the like**, **and although there are tzaddikim who are moved with pain at the lack of honour and other desirable things of this world, the pain is very easily set aside.**

<!--cont-->**For it did not touch the soul so much; from this it follows that they have no great will and pleasure in it, only a very little, therefore the pain is small and easily set aside, since the pain comes in exactly the same measure as the pleasure, as explained above.**

[28.3]{.pnum} **And there are complete tzaddikim who are not moved at all by a loss of money or a fall in honour, and even a lessening of the flow of children, life and sustenance they accept with love and complete will, as is known**[Children, life and sustenance (*banei, chayei umezonei*): three things on which a person depends in this world, in the words of the Talmud (Moed Katan 28a).]{.fn}; **if so, they necessarily have no will or longing at all, not even a little, in the matters of this world.**

<!--cont-->**And therefore they are not moved with pain and affliction at all by the opposite, at its absence, etc.; to the point that in all the things of this world, even when he wants something, if it is done against his will he does not mind at all, for he feels no pain at all at the opposite of his will, because this will does not come inwardly at all, only very externally, so that affliction and pain do not apply to its opposite**: even a tzaddik wants things, but his will in them is only external, so he feels no pain when they are absent.

[28.4]{.pnum} **Which is not so when he feels even a little pain at the opposite of his will: then he necessarily has an inner will according to the measure of this pain, as explained above; so it emerges that complete tzaddikim, who are stripped of all will for the physicality of this world, feel no pain at all even when things are done against their will, because they have no wish or will at all for the pleasures of this world.**

<!--cont-->**And this is called equanimity, for whom yes and no are exactly equal, in one equality, like wealth and honour, at whose absence he feels no pain at all, because yes and no are equal to him, as he said, “G‑d gave and G‑d has taken”** (Job 1:21): **so the giving is equal to the taking; and therefore one must bless over the bad exactly as one blesses over the good, in one equality, etc.; and the wise will understand:** (Berachot 54a).

[29.2]{.pnum} **For as long as something of the *Chayah* and *Yechidah* still shines in the animal soul, it is called a trial, when one transforms and nullifies this will because the divine will prevails, as in “and G‑d tested Abraham,” that he should slaughter his only son for the sake of the divine will, and the like**[Trial (*nisayon*): a state in which two wills stand against each other, and either may prevail. ### 36. What peace is

[36.1]{.pnum} **The explanation of the matter is understood from all the above, from the main difference between the surrender of the will that comes through war and essential self-sacrifice: in self-sacrifice through war, one can at any rate speak of pain and affliction in what is against his natural will, only he gives himself up because the divine will prevails, etc.; if so, this is not called peace**: one who wins a war still feels what he gives up, and so he has no peace.

[36.2]{.pnum} **For the whole matter of peace is one who sits in tranquility, with no opponent at all from whom he would receive pain and affliction; and when he has no affliction from anything done against his will, then it is called peace, because the foreign will is nullified in essence, without feeling affliction and pain in his soul at the absence of his foreign will, etc.; and this is the arousal of the essence of “with all your might,” explained above, where every nullification of the foreign will is not called affliction at all but pleasure and joy.**

<!--cont-->**If so, the divine soul has no opponent at all, for the opponent is nullified in its very essence, without war at all.** The test of peace is thus the same as the test of equanimity (chapter 8): the absence of pain.

[36.3]{.pnum} **(Because in the *Yechidah* actual divine light shines, which is called the image of G‑d, as explained above; and then of itself every source of foreign will in the animal soul is nullified, like melting wax, etc., as explained above, for it counts as nothing, etc.; and this is the peace that is above war, like what will be in the time to come, when there will be nullification in essence in all creatures, even in the evil of *noga*, because of the great revelation of G‑dliness**[*Kelipat noga* (the glowing husk): the *kelipah* in which good and evil are mixed; the permitted matters of this world come from it, and from it too comes the animal soul of a Jew. In the time to come, when the divine light is revealed, even the evil in it will be nullified of itself.]{.fn}, **as it is written, “they shall not harm nor destroy; the lion shall eat straw like the ox,” etc., and of the nations it is written, “and all the nations shall stream to it,” etc.** (Isaiah 2:2), **in nullification of themselves, not through war at all, as it is written, “and they shall go into the caves of the rocks for fear of G‑d,” etc.** (Isaiah 2:19), **and not as in the days of David and Joshua, who conquered by war, etc.**

<!--cont-->**And this is because with Moshiach there will be a revelation of the inwardness of *Atik* through the first three, etc., called *Gulgalta*, where the very essence of the infinite light shines, and it is above simple will, the source of all will, that is, entirely above will, etc.**[The inwardness of *Atik*; *Gulgalta*: *Atik Yomin* (the Ancient of Days) is the inwardness of the *Keter* (Crown), above will. *Gulgalta* is the highest of the first three levels of the *Keter*, in which, in the words of the discourse, “the very essence of the infinite light shines.” In the time to come this level will be revealed, and therefore the nullification will come of itself, without war.]{.fn} **as it is written in Daniel, “and *Atik* sat… His kingdom an everlasting kingdom,” etc., without any change at all**, “and the Ancient of Days sat” (Daniel 7:9, 7:27), **and as explained elsewhere regarding the seventy years of David, etc., which is the main difference between David and King Moshiach.**

<!--cont-->**And of this David said, “He redeemed my soul in peace,” the soul of David as it will be in the time to come, at the level of the first three of *Atik Yomin* and *Adam Kadmon*, etc., as it is written, “and shall be very high”** (Isaiah 52:13), **which is “with all your might,” explained above; and the wise will understand.)** King David conquered his land through wars; and in this verse he speaks of his soul as it will be in the time to come, in peace.

[36.4]{.pnum} **Which is not so with the surrender of the will, where at any rate the word affliction applies to the animal soul: this is not peace for the divine soul either; for although the divine soul itself is free of every foreign will, without feeling affliction, etc., still the very toil of this war, to transform the animal soul too, counts as affliction for the divine soul, for it has an opponent at any rate, only it transforms it (and moreover, in the wrestling of the war the divine soul is necessarily soiled a little with the filthy garments of the animal soul until it transforms it, as in the analogy of one who wrestles with a filthy one, and like the garments in which one cooks, etc., as explained above regarding refinements, that the refiner is soiled with the garments of what is being refined, as it is written, “and I have stained all My raiment,” etc.; and the wise will understand).** Even the divine soul, which does not want evil at all, is not at peace as long as an opponent faces it: the very toil of the war is a pain to it, and the struggle soils it with the garments of the animal soul (chapter 4).

### 37. Redemption: enemies become friends

[37.1]{.pnum} **And this is the matter of redemption, as it says, “He redeemed my soul in peace,” etc., like one who redeems and rescues: the divine soul is rescued from the opposition of the animal soul in the way of peace, without war at all, because the opponent is nullified in essence, as explained above; and likewise what it says, “Into Your hand I entrust my spirit,” etc.** (Psalms 31:6): **its initials spell *be’er*, a well, which is essential self-sacrifice, as explained above**: the initial letters of *beyadcha afkid ruchi* spell *be’er*, **of which he said, “You have redeemed me” from the externals, in the way of peace, without war, because they are entirely nullified**, as the verse continues: “You have redeemed me, O G‑d, G‑d of truth.” **(And this is what it says, “and you shall know that your tent is at peace,” etc.** (Job 5:24), **“may there be peace within your ramparts, tranquility,” etc.** (Psalms 122:7), **and likewise what it says, “and great shall be the peace of your children”** (Isaiah 54:13), **that Torah scholars increase peace** (Berachot 64a), **and likewise the matter of making peace in the household above** (Sanhedrin 99b): **all this is through essential self-sacrifice, in which the opponent is nullified in essence and no war at all is needed.**
::::

<!--cont-->**And likewise through the study of Torah, for “through Wisdom they are refined,” with the nullification of evil in essence, as in the time to come, of which it is written, “and all flesh shall see”** (Isaiah 40:5), **which is the seeing of Wisdom, etc., “and every creature shall know,” etc.** (from the prayers of the Days of Awe), **and so it was in the days of Solomon, when all the wise men of the nations were nullified in essence before the wisdom of Solomon, not through war.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore he was called Shlomo, for there was peace (*shalom*) in his days, etc., as explained elsewhere; and the wise will understand.)** “Through Wisdom they are refined”: wisdom refines without war, for evil is nullified before it of itself.

[37.2]{.pnum} **Which is not so through war, as it says, “Arise, O G‑d, and let Your enemies be scattered,” when the Ark journeyed, etc.: although they melt like wax, etc., as it says, “shall perish,” etc., “Your enemies, O G‑d, shall perish,” etc., they are at any rate called enemies of G‑d, only they have been utterly subdued, with a transformation of essence, etc.; but in the way of peace they become complete friends and are not called enemies of G‑d at all, and need no destruction at all.**

<!--cont-->**On the contrary, it is written, “for then I will turn to the peoples,” etc., “and all the nations shall stream to it,” etc., “and strangers shall stand,” etc.** (Isaiah 61:5), **“and kings shall be your nursemaids,” and the like.)** (Isaiah 49:23). This is the difference between the two ways: war destroys the enemies; peace makes them friends. **And by way of analogy, as when a person is rid of his enemy in the way of peace, when he is turned into a friend through the strength of true nullification, as it was with Jacob and Esau, as it is written, “and Esau ran toward him and kissed him,” etc.** (Genesis 33:4) **(and first it says, “and a man wrestled with him,” that is, the angel of Esau above, and afterward below, of itself, he was turned into a friend.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore “and he kissed him,” etc.; and the wise will understand; and this is what Amasai said to David, “Yours, David,” etc., “peace, peace,” etc.)** (I Chronicles 12:19). After Jacob defeated the angel of Esau above, Esau himself, below, became a friend of his own accord.

### 38. Why redemption is needed

[38.1]{.pnum} **But one must still understand why redemption is needed, since there is no opponent at all; and likewise the matter of “redeems and rescues,” and it is written, “and the redeemed of G‑d shall return”** (Isaiah 35:10): **what is this redemption?** To redeem is to give something in order to bring a thing out of another’s domain; and if there is no opponent, from whom is it redeemed? **But the matter is known: because of the root of the hold of the *kelipah* on the side of holiness, a ransom must be given them, which is a small thing in exchange for a great one, as in “and they shall give each man a ransom for his soul,” etc.** (Exodus 30:12), **and likewise with the Hebrew maidservant, of whom it says, “if he has not designated her, he shall let her be redeemed”** (Exodus 21:8), **and as is known, the divine soul is called the King’s daughter**, the daughter of the King, **and this is what it says, “and if a man sells his daughter as a maidservant”** (Exodus 21:7): **to transform the animal soul, “she shall not go out” through self-sacrifice “as the servants go out,” the soul of *noga*.**
::::

<!--cont-->**But by designation or redemption, as it says, “He redeemed my soul in peace,” etc.**: the divine soul went down into the body like a maidservant sold in order to transform the animal soul, and it does not go out as servants do, but through redemption. **(And an example of this is the sending away of the goat to Azazel, etc.** (Leviticus 16:21–22), **and likewise it is written, “and He will redeem Israel,” etc.)** (Psalms 130:8).

[38.2]{.pnum} **This redemption is so that the animal soul will not join the divine soul as it rises above, etc., because it is said, “peace, peace, to the far and to the near”** (Isaiah 57:19): **to the far who has become near, etc. (but through peace, which is a joining for the moment, by this it will be redeemed to be absorbed in the body of the King, etc., alone, with no stranger with it, etc.; and the wise will understand).** Peace brings the far near, and therefore the divine soul must afterward be separated from it through redemption, so that it may rise and be included in the King alone, “with no stranger with it.”

### 39. The Ten Martyrs

[39.1]{.pnum} **And a true example of essential self-sacrifice, in which there is no affliction at all at the absence of all the life of the animal soul, etc., we find in the Ten Martyrs, like Rabbi Akiva, whose soul departed at “One”: even under harsh afflictions, like iron combs, he did not feel it as affliction at all at that time, when he was drawing out the word “One”; for had he felt it, his soul would not have drawn out “One” until it departed and was included in the One, etc.** (Berachot 61b).
::::

[39.2]{.pnum} **And this is what Rabbi Akiva said, “When will it come to my hand that I may fulfil it?”, for this is essential self-sacrifice: he did not trust himself, when he said “with all your might,” regarding the complete nullification of the will, so that he would feel none of the afflictions of the body, which is what is called peace, as explained above, until actual self-sacrifice came to his hand**: Rabbi Akiva did not trust that he had reached the level of “with all your might,” a nullification without pain, until he stood in it in fact.

[39.3]{.pnum} **And so were all the Ten Martyrs: they felt no pain in their animal soul from the killing, for their self-sacrifice was in joy and pleasure, because all the pleasures of this world occupied no place for them, etc., since they had only one will and not two wills, etc., as explained above.** **And this is the main advantage of their self-sacrifice over all the actual self-sacrifice of others, although many righteous of the world were killed for the sanctification of the Name.**

<!--cont-->**But only of the Ten Martyrs, “no creature,” etc.** (Pesachim 50a), **as is written in *Etz Chaim*, that they raise *mayin nukvin* to *Abba* and *Ima*, etc., until the days of Moshiach, etc., and as explained elsewhere**[*Mayin nukvin* (“feminine waters”): an ascent from below that arouses the flow from above. In the writings of the Arizal it is explained that the souls of the Ten Martyrs raise *mayin nukvin* to *Abba* and *Ima* (Father and Mother) until the days of Moshiach.]{.fn}.

### 40. “From the battle against me”

[40.1]{.pnum} **And this is “He redeemed my soul in peace from the battle against me (*mikrav li*)”: that there be no battle and war, for war makes a closeness for the moment, etc., as in the analogy of those who wrestle together, etc.; and this is “*mikrav li*,” a word of closeness and a word of war, and therefore war is called *kraba*, etc.**: in Hebrew *krav* means both nearness and battle, and the whole discourse explains why: war brings close.
::::

[40.2]{.pnum} **But in the way of peace, which is “your might,” essential self-sacrifice, the animal soul too is nullified in essence, as explained above, and not that it will thereby join the divine soul; for if so, the question returns to its place, that this peace makes more closeness than war.** **But this closeness is a true closeness, like faithful friends, one of whom is included and mingled in the other, etc.; and nevertheless, after this peace, the divine soul is separated through redemption.**

<!--cont-->**For G‑d redeems it, as explained above, and as Abraham said to Lot, “please separate from me,” etc.** (Genesis 13:9), **for certainly when Michael, the high priest, offers souls on the altar, no foreign thing will be mixed into this offering, and especially leaven, etc.:** (Menachot 110a; Leviticus 2:11: “for no leaven and no honey shall you burn”). In peace there is true closeness, like the love of friends; and yet, when the soul rises and is offered above, it rises alone, unmixed.

### 41. “For among the many”: the general surrounding light

[41.1]{.pnum} **(12) “For among the many,” etc.: this gives a great reason for the beginning of his words, when he said “He redeemed my soul in peace”; he goes on to explain why He redeemed in peace and not in war: “for among the many,” etc.** **“Among the many” means within the many: the surrounding light of the *Yechidah*, called the general surrounding light, which includes all the particular surrounding lights, etc.; and “among the many” means what is hidden and concealed within the many, which is the most general level, surrounding all of them equally, and not in a particular way with differences of level, as at the giving of the Torah, where it is written “and Israel camped (*vayichan*),” in the singular** (Exodus 19:2: “as one man with one heart,” in Rashi’s words), **and likewise “bless us, all of us as one,” etc.**, in the words of the prayer, **and it is the level of *Gulgalta*, the first three, *Keter, Chochmah, Binah*, which includes every detail of the souls of Israel, which are in the form of a complete stature.**
::::

<!--cont-->**And this light shines in all the singular few of every generation, etc.** The *Yechidah* of every soul is part of the general *Yechidah* of the whole Congregation of Israel, and there all are equal.

[41.2]{.pnum} **This is the level of Moses, who said, “six hundred thousand on foot is the people in whose midst I am,” etc.** (Numbers 11:21): **six hundred thousand understandings, each according to his grasp and according to his will, shining from the *Chayah* and the love of “with all your soul”; but in the *Yechidah*, the surrounding of the surrounding, all are equal and are called one man, as it is written, “and Israel camped,” the level of *Gulgalta*, etc., which is entirely above will.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore they said “we will do” and afterward “we will hear,” etc., as explained above; and in this war does not apply at all, as explained above**: in the *Chayah* there are six hundred thousand understandings, each according to his grasp and will; in the *Yechidah* there is no distinction. **And for this he gave the reason: “for among the many,” which includes all six hundred thousand, “they were with me,” which is the radiance of the very essence of the infinite light, blessed be He, in *Gulgalta*, which is called the general *Keter*.**

<!--cont-->**Therefore He redeemed in peace the soul of the totality of the souls of Israel, that they will need no war at all; and the wise will understand.** Since the light in David is the general light of all Israel, it is this light that redeems his soul in peace.

### 43. The plain sense: with ten, and the inwardness of Wisdom

[43.1]{.pnum} **But a verse does not depart from its plain meaning** (Shabbat 63a), **which implies that because He was with him among the many specifically, therefore He redeemed my soul in peace; and this is for the reason they said, “in every gathering of ten,” etc.; and even Moses had to pray with ten, and there was a difference between his prayer alone and his prayer with the congregation, etc., for the reason explained above**: in its plain sense, David was redeemed in peace because he prayed with the many, and the Shechinah rests on ten.

<!--cont-->**And this is only the essential self-sacrifice explained above, and not as it is in the inner light of their souls alone, as explained above**[Being and nothing (*yesh* and *ayin*): “being” is what feels itself an existence of its own; “nothing” is what is nullified and does not feel itself. ## Summary

[Summary.1]{.pnum} The discourse distinguishes two ways in the service of prayer. In the way of war, the divine soul struggles with the animal soul in order to transform it to the will of G‑d. The struggle draws the two close to each other, and so precisely at the time of prayer stray thoughts grow strong and draw life from holiness, and even the victor is soiled. This war belongs to the inner light, to *Nefesh, Ruach* and *Neshamah*, and to the *Chayah* that shines in the mind and heart. At most it reaches the transforming of the will, “the nullification of the minority in the majority,” and therefore it is only for the moment: when the strength of prayer withdraws, the foreign will returns and reawakens. This is “the surrender of the will,” “with all your heart and with all your soul.”

[Summary.2]{.pnum} Above it is the surrender of the soul itself, from the *Yechidah*, “with all your might.” The essence of the soul is not a will but the source of all will; when it is nullified, there is nothing left for a foreign will to come from, and the nullification depends on no prevailing power and does not change. Its sign is equanimity: one who feels no pain at all when something of this world is taken from him has no pleasure in it. So in the love of David and Jonathan, in which one who gives himself for his friend feels no pain, and so in the Ten Martyrs, whose self-sacrifice was in joy.

[Summary.3]{.pnum} This is “He redeemed my soul in peace”: peace, for there is no opponent; redemption, so that the divine soul rises alone; “from the battle against me,” without the closeness of war. And the reason, “for among the many they were with me”: the *Yechidah* is the general surrounding light of all Israel, in which all are “one man,” and it is revealed in the singular few of every generation and in the congregation that prays with ten.
