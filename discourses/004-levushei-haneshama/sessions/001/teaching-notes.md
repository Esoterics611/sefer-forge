## a. What this part teaches

The introduction puts the maamar in context. Following the Zohar ("אברהם זקן
בא בימים"), the earlier maamarim taught that the days of a life in Torah and
mitzvot make garments for the soul, in which it enjoys the radiance of the
Shechinah. Here, in his own words, the Alter Rebbe makes precise what those
garments are and what Torah and mitzvot have to do with them: the Zohar
sounds as if they are the garments, and so last forever. He brings Etz
Chaim's account of the garments of the נר"ן. The speaker gives the subject's
worth for daily service, and above all for prayer. He also previews a point
taken up later: psychology studies the animal soul, one of the two צלמים;
the divine soul is the other; neither is the Arizal's נר"ן. The parts that
follow work this out.
