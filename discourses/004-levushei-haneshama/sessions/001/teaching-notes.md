## a. What this part teaches

The introduction puts the maamar in context. Following the Zohar ("אברהם זקן
בא בימים"), the earlier maamarim taught that the days of a life in Torah and
mitzvot make garments for the soul, in which it enjoys the radiance of the
Shechinah. Here, in his own words, the Alter Rebbe makes precise what those
garments are and what Torah and mitzvot have to do with them: the Zohar
sounds as if they are the garments, and so last forever. He brings Etz
Chaim's account of the garments of the נר"ן. The speaker gives the subject's
worth for daily service, and above all for prayer. He also previews a point
taken up later: psychology studies the animal soul, one of the two צלמים;
the divine soul is the other; neither is the Arizal's נר"ן. The parts that
follow work this out.

Part ג' (לבאר ענין הצלם.m4a, added 2026-11-09) gives the definition the
introduction promises ("להלן יתבאר מהם הצלמים"). In the Arizal's writings the
צלם is the intermediary that joins the נר"ן to the body. The speaker
illustrates it from Jung: a stack of structures. At its base are the genes in
the cells, which dictate the instincts; a person's deeds act back on both.
On the instincts, information from the surroundings builds personality
patterns (king, warrior, lover, sage). Genes and instincts build complexes,
half natural and half shaped by surroundings and behaviour. The patterns
combine into one personality. All of this is below the conscious thought,
speech and action, the garments of the soul proper. The personality speaks
through them, and all it hears, thinks and lives is written in some "air". He
proposes that this air is the צלם. The two צלמים are the good and the evil
inclination; man's thoughts, the angels' speech (הערה ב'), receive here from
these two structures.
