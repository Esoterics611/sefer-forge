# The Garments of the Soul

[An introduction to Kabbalah and Chassidus: the soul, its garments, and the secret of Torah and mitzvot]{.subtitle}

## One hour

The Mishnah teaches: "Better is one hour of repentance and good deeds in this world than all the life of the World to Come."[Mishnah, Avot 4:17.]{.fn} The statement is surprising. The World to Come is the reward, the goal to which every deed is directed. How can one hour of work, here, in the body, amid the distractions of this world, be better than it?

The Alter Rebbe, author of the Tanya, answers this question in a discourse in his book *Torah Or*, and adds to it a gloss in his own hand, "the words of our master himself," in which he opens the inner map of the soul as the writings of the Arizal describe it.[Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, the discourse beginning "To understand the saying of our sages, Better is one hour," 15c–17a, and the gloss there.]{.fn} From this map we learn one of the foundations of Kabbalah and Chassidus: the soul has garments, and Torah and mitzvot are the craft by which these garments are woven.

## What is a human being?

Kabbalah distinguishes three things in a person: the soul itself, the body, and what stands between them. The soul is "the form," of which it is said: "The form is the essence, which is called *man*; and it is Yechidah, Chayah, and Nefesh-Ruach-Neshamah."[Etz Chaim, Gate 26 (the Gate of the Image), ch. 1.]{.fn} It has five levels: *Nefesh*, *Ruach*, *Neshamah*, *Chayah* and *Yechidah*. The body, "the matter," is the vessel. And the soul does not enter the body directly: "It is impossible for the Nefesh, the Ruach or the Neshamah to be clothed within the body except by means of this *tzelem* (image)."[Etz Chaim, Gate 26, ch. 2.]{.fn}

The *tzelem* is the garment. It is the intermediary between the light and the vessel; without it the soul could not dwell in the body, nor stand before a higher light. It has an inner part and a surrounding part: some of it enters and clothes the soul from within, and some remains outside and encircles it. The surrounding part is called "*chashmal*, whose numerical value equals *malbush*, garment."[Ibid.]{.fn}

## Three garments: thought, speech and action

Chassidus brings these ideas into life. In the Tanya the Alter Rebbe writes: "Every divine soul has, in addition, three garments: thought, speech and action of the 613 commandments of the Torah." When a person fulfils mitzvot in action, studies Torah in speech, and grasps of it in thought all that he can, "all the 613 limbs of his soul are clothed in the 613 commandments of the Torah."[Tanya, Likkutei Amarim, ch. 4.]{.fn}

And Kabbalah reveals which garment is woven from each of them, and in which world: "Through a person's intention in Torah and mitzvot, the garment of the Neshamah is completed"; through the Torah "the *nogah* of Yetzirah is refined: the garment of the Ruach"; "and through practical mitzvot the *nogah* of Asiyah is refined and becomes the garment of the Nefesh."[Etz Chaim, Gate 49 (the Gate of Kelipat Nogah), ch. 5.]{.fn} Thought, speech and action are not only tools of service. They become threads in the garment of the soul, each in a different world.

## Four worlds, three garments

Kabbalah describes reality as four worlds, one below the other: *Atzilut* (emanation), *Beriah* (creation), *Yetzirah* (formation) and *Asiyah* (action). And a person does not stand in the lowest world alone. So it is with "the lower man, who includes all the worlds of Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah": he has form, *tzelem* and vessels in each of them.[Etz Chaim, Gate 26, ch. 1.]{.fn} The garments of the soul are therefore garments in the worlds: "from the *chashmal* of Beriah, the garment of the Neshamah; from the *chashmal* of Yetzirah, the garment of the Ruach; from the *chashmal* of Asiyah, the garment of the Nefesh."[Etz Chaim, Gate 49, ch. 4.]{.fn} Together they form one picture:

| The garment is woven from | The part of the soul it clothes | The world |
|---|---|---|
| intention, thought | Neshamah | Beriah |
| Torah, speech | Ruach | Yetzirah |
| practical mitzvot, action | Nefesh | Asiyah |

## The days that become a garment

The Zohar paints this in a living image. Each day of a person's life stands on its own, and at the end of his life the days come before the King. If he was worthy, the soul "rises and enters into those days, and they are garments of honour in which his soul is clothed": the days themselves are the robes the soul puts on.[Zohar I, 224a.]{.fn} Of Abraham it is said that he "came with his days": into those very days of his "he entered and was clothed in them," and nothing was missing from the garment.[Ibid.]{.fn} "And in that garment they merit to delight in the delight of the World to Come."[Zohar I, 224b.]{.fn}

So no day is empty. Every day is a thread; and every mitzvah, every word of Torah, every moment of intention, is the weaving itself.

## A garment that fell and waits

And here is the great novelty. The garments are not newly created by the mitzvot. The first man had garments from the start: "the garments of Adam, garments of light, and they were of the *chashmal*."[Etz Chaim, Gate 49, ch. 4.]{.fn} Through the sin they fell and were mixed into *kelipat nogah*, the husk in which good and evil are mingled, and became "garments of skin."[Etz Chaim, Gate 41 (the Gate of Chashmal), ch. 1.]{.fn} In the words of Etz Chaim: "These garments... it does not mean that they are made anew through Torah and mitzvot; rather, they are clarified from the evil with which they were mixed through the sin of Adam."[Etz Chaim, Gate 49, ch. 5; with these words the gloss in Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, 16c, begins.]{.fn}

That is: in a mitzvah a person does not produce something foreign to him. He finds within the world what was his from the start, and draws it out of the mixture. The soul's garments of light lie scattered in physical things, in the leather of tefillin, in the wool of tzitzit, in the coin given to charity, and they wait for someone to clarify them.

## Why the soul descended

From here comes the answer to the great question: why did the soul descend into the body? Not for its own sake. "The form needs no repair at all, and it did not need to be clothed in *tzelem* and matter except to draw light into them to repair them... for this is the reason for the soul's descent into this world: to repair and to clarify, like the exile of the Shechinah."[Etz Chaim, Gate 26, ch. 1.]{.fn} Just as the Shechinah descends among the husks to clarify the sparks from there, so the soul descends into the body to clarify its garments.

And the Alter Rebbe sharpens this: the soul's descent into this world, "into the physical body to fulfil practical Torah and mitzvot," "is like the exile of the Shechinah, and it is only to clarify and repair their own garments." Therefore "it is a greater obligation upon the Nefesh, Ruach and Neshamah to clarify and repair their garments, which cannot be done by others."[Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, 16c, in the gloss.]{.fn} There are tasks that others can do for us; the garment of each soul no one else can weave.

## Why the reward is a garment

"The soul does not rise to be seen before the Holy King until it merits to be clothed in a garment from above."[Zohar II, 229b.]{.fn} Divine light without a garment is like sunlight to the eye: no one can stand it. The garment is what lets the soul see and delight. So the discourse explains the cloud into which Moses entered: the cloud's root is "from a very exalted level of Godliness," and it became "a garment for Moses, through which he could apprehend Godliness." "So too," when a person engages in Torah and mitzvot, "garments are made for his soul... through which the soul, which is a created being, can apprehend the radiance of the Shechinah."[Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, 16a–b.]{.fn} That is why the reward of the mitzvot is a garment: it is what allows a created being to receive the light.

## "Better is one hour"

Now we can return to the first question. The Upper Garden of Eden is "the world of Beriah, called the Upper Garden of Eden."[Tanya, Likkutei Amarim, ch. 39.]{.fn} But the root of Torah and mitzvot is higher: "Torah and practical mitzvot, whose root is in the Supreme Will, in His wisdom... which are higher than the radiance of the Shechinah."[Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, 16a.]{.fn} And the gloss adds: "The Upper Garden of Eden is of Beriah. This explains the Mishnah, Better is one hour, for the union of Ze'ir Anpin and Nukva is in Atzilut, and the mitzvot draw down the 'male waters' of Atzilut." And this holds even for the simplest of the simple: "even one who has only a Nefesh of Asiyah," his mitzvot rise from world to world up to Atzilut.[Torah Or, Chayei Sarah, 16d, in the gloss; Etz Chaim, Gate 39 (the Gate of the Feminine and Masculine Waters), discourse 11.]{.fn} And so in the Tanya: in the study of Torah "the unity and the light of the Infinite, blessed be He, spreads from Atzilut down to Asiyah."[Tanya, Likkutei Amarim, ch. 40, in the gloss.]{.fn}

The Garden of Eden is delight in the radiance of the Shechinah; a mitzvah is a bond with the Supreme Will itself. That is why one hour of mitzvot in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come. The reward is a garment of light; the deed is oneness with the One who commanded it.

## A life of weaving

Here is a new view of a life of Torah and mitzvot. The mitzvot are not a list of duties imposed from outside. They are the weaving of the soul's garment out of the materials of this world. In the writings of the Arizal, tzitzit and tefillin themselves are called "the garments of a person in the World to Come": "tzitzit in Yetzirah, and tefillin, which are more inward, in Beriah."[Etz Chaim, Gate 41, ch. 3.]{.fn} The body is not a partition between a person and the Divine but the loom on which the garment is woven. Every thought, every word and every deed of this day is a thread in the garment in which the soul will stand before the King; and that thread can be woven only here, and only by its owner.

## For study and discussion

1. If the garments are not created anew but "clarified," what changes in our relation to the physical world: to the object with which a mitzvah is done, to the money given to charity, to the body itself?
2. Thought, speech and action: in which of the three is it easier for us to serve, and in which harder? What does it mean that each of them weaves a garment for a different part of the soul?
3. "Which cannot be done by others": what is there in each person that only he or she can do? How does this change the feeling of a "small" mitzvah?
4. The Zohar says that the days themselves become a garment. What would a whole day look like in which every hour is a thread in the garment?
