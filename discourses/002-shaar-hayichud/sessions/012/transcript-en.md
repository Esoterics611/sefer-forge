## Two Levels in Understanding

> And from all this, the matter of contemplation in grasping Godliness will be understood: there are two levels, Understanding and lower Understanding. Understanding is the very grasp of Godliness in every detail: one contemplates how it is and what it is, and stands upon the matter with much study, in all the details of its parts.

That is: we have explained Understanding, and the depth within Understanding, which is study; and the emotions within Understanding, where the idea is already filled with letters, and how I relate to this idea, which is already the matter of the emotions within the mind; and on to lower Understanding. Lower Understanding, we said, is the Kingship of lower Understanding, which is the letters within the mind. There is the welling up of the idea, which is the fiftieth gate of Understanding. There is the spreading out of the idea in length and breadth, until it is a full idea one can relate to, which is the emotions of Understanding, the seven lower attributes of Understanding. And Kingship, which is lower Understanding. Lower Understanding, we said, is the seven lower attributes of Understanding: the idea already clothes itself in thought, though the thought is not yet clothed in letters; but it is already a thought I can relate to with my personality, still in the subconscious layers, but already belonging to the soul of the person, or rather to the person's personality; until it reaches the Kingship of lower Understanding, which is already the letters of thought.

So all of this was an introduction, to understand contemplation in grasping Godliness, which is on the level of Understanding and on the level of lower Understanding. He begins to explain that Understanding is the very grasp of Godliness, as we said: that is, when you already grasp some point.

## Contemplating the One Who Fills All Worlds

The Rebbe continues:

> As in the particular grasp of the One who fills all worlds, which is the bringing of something into being from nothing, called the power of the Maker within the made.

That is: in the next lines the Rebbe gives us an example of how to contemplate, of what the attribute of Understanding is. He contemplates an idea of the holy Zohar called the One who fills all worlds. The next lines do not come only to present to us the expression and the idea of the One who fills all worlds, from the Zohar; they come to give us an example of how a grasp of Godliness takes shape. The Rebbe continues:

> As in the particular grasp of the One who fills all worlds, which is the bringing of something into being from nothing, called the power of the Maker within the made. It is from the divine Name that means Lord, or the Name of God that is a word of power, as is known. Now, there are in this many particular distinctions in the outer ways of the matter, until the inside of this matter is grasped in its own inwardness, how it is in itself.

That is: the Rebbe brings us an example here: how the One who fills all worlds brings about how creation comes into being, something from nothing; and he brings the power of the Maker within the made, and the Name that means Lord and the Name of God. This, too, is understood as an example of the breadth of the river: there are very, very many details. And after that there is the length of the river, which is how the matter of the One who fills all worlds can be explained even to a child, through parables and examples. So we have all the details and all the parallel things, such as the Names, Lord and God, and the bringing into being of something from nothing: all of these are details and aspects of the idea of the One who fills all worlds. After that you have the length, which is all the parables, until it is understood and the idea is brought near. And through the length and the breadth, you reach the very point.

After you have explained the idea of the One who fills all worlds in all its details, and clothed it in parables, in length and breadth, you reach the very essence of the One who fills all worlds. You cannot clothe it in words; but you have acquired it, some grasp, some relationship to it. As it is written in the Talmud: "A child does not know how to say 'Father' until he tastes the taste of grain." Although the child does not understand what a father is, nor the chain of how he came to be, he knows in his heart that this is Father, with all that Father means. Even at eighty, when he explains the essence of what a father is, he will still be at the same inner point of the essence of the matter that he was at as a baby. So it is here, in understanding the One who fills all worlds. If you reach the essence of the matter, you have a bond to it; you understand it, even without the garments of the intellect. That is contemplation. And when you reach this level, then the meanings are grasped; and then, when you open the Zohar and study the words of the Zohar, the whole length and breadth of the river flows of itself, and wells up within you yourself as you study.

Back to the words of the Rebbe:

> And this is what is called the depth of the idea.

So: until you have reached the inner essence, how it is in itself, this is called the depth of the idea, as said above, study being the vessel for coming to the depth. So the study we spoke of above is the vessel for reaching the depth. You cannot reach the depth of the idea until you have understood it in length and breadth.

## The Life of the Soul in the Body

> As for length: bringing this matter near to the mind in various known parables, until this thing is very near in his mind, in a physical garment, until even a child can grasp it, as said above.

> And breadth: to widen this thing as it is, in several different ways, and not in one way only; as in the parable of the life of the soul in the body, which is not only a spreading out from the essence, as is known.

That is: now the Rebbe brings us an example of the breadth too: taking the matter of the One who fills all worlds and setting it parallel to the life of the soul in the body. As is known: as the soul fills the body, so the Holy One, blessed be He, fills the world, which is the One who fills all worlds. And what is this filling? What is the life of the soul in the body? That it is only a spreading out from the essence. The Rebbe continues.

Again: here in the Gate of Unity the Rebbe both explains to us ideas of Hasidic teaching and of the inner Torah in general, and also explains to us how to contemplate them. That is why it is the key, the key to all the rest of the Hasidic teaching of Chabad. The Rebbe continues:

> There are very many ways in the spreading out from the essence, and also in the revealing of the hidden: like the movement of a living being, and like the letters of speech, or like reasoning wisely from the essence of wisdom.

All of these are parables for the breadth, with all the details and all the parallels. You truly have the details; you have the parables and the examples, which are the length; and you have the parallels, which are the breadth. The Rebbe continues:

> Or like the revealing of sight in the eye, or like the revealing of a flame from the coal.

These are all parables and parallels for the One who fills all worlds.

> And all this is called the revealing of the hidden. Which is not so in the parable of the radiance of the sun, or the light of a candle, or the power of the maker, which is called spreading out: all of this is within the matter itself, only widened.

That is: all the details we have said here are all present in the very matter of the One who fills all worlds, for example; only through contemplation you widen them.

## Length and Breadth: Brevity Is the Opposite

The Rebbe continues:

> And the opposite of this is brevity: in one way only, and glanced over briefly in his mind, without spreading out to widen it at all. But as this thing is grasped in its depth, in its essence, after all the many explanations and images of the parables, that is called the depth and the core of the idea, as said above. And precisely after the length and breadth of the parables, with much study, one comes to the depth that is understood from them.

That means: the opposite of this is when you look at the thing briefly. "Ah, the One who fills all worlds; there is nothing but Him; fine." We have thrown out a slogan; and without going into all the details, you have not uncovered this idea, until it wells up in the soul, and wells up into feelings, arousal and new ideas. These flowings will not rise, will not spring up, if you do not stop on the matter. When something is said briefly and in a rush, one does not reach the depth of its meaning. The Rebbe continues:

> After clothing its outside in all the much study; and the understanding will understand.

What he explains is that the whole length and breadth of the river, which is all the details and parables we spoke of, are the outside: they are all garments of the essence of the matter. So after you have contemplated all the garments, there, in the end, you can also reach the essence of the matter.

## The Crown, Wisdom and Understanding Within Understanding

The Rebbe continues:

> As written above, in all the details of the Crown, Wisdom and Understanding within Understanding: all of it will be found in such a particular grasp, and so in every particular grasp of Godliness.

Here he hints that the very body of the river is the Crown, Wisdom and Understanding within Understanding: these are the first three of Understanding. We did not speak of this before. The Crown, Wisdom and Understanding, we said, the first three, are Understanding: the matter of its essential part. There is essence and there is spreading out. The seven lower ones are a matter of spreading out, of revealing the essence. There, as we already said, is lower Understanding, in which there is already something to relate to. Since here there is not the essence of the idea: without clothing it in parables and details, you cannot touch it. So the first three, the Crown, Wisdom and Understanding within Understanding, are themselves the very point; and the seven lower ones, or lower Understanding, are the revealing of the matters and their clothing.

## Know This Day: Knowledge That Wakes the Heart

The Rebbe continues:

> Knowledge is recognition and feeling, as of an idea one is bound to. And of this it is said, "Know this day, and lay it to your heart," and "Know the God of your father." This is precisely in this grasp of the One who fills all worlds, as is known, which precisely brings to the depth of everything, that is, to the depth of Wisdom, which "is found from nothing," as said above.

That is: this is what is called Knowledge. Knowledge is, as you go on: "know." Knowledge is a union of Wisdom and Understanding. Knowledge is also binding oneself. An idea in itself you cannot bind yourself to: it is cold, it is not bound to you, you do not understand it; it is not even clothed in letters. The idea is clothed in Understanding, and there, in Understanding, Understanding truly clothes it in letters, in length and breadth, until you can relate to it. And the union of the two together, the outcome and lower Understanding, gives birth to Knowledge. And then the thing belongs to you. When you understand the matter of the One who fills all worlds, and, let us say, grasp the very point, which is called Knowledge, then through the Knowledge by which you reach it, it wakes the heart. That is "Know this day, and lay it to your heart": this Knowledge stirs the heart. From the heart it sends blood to all the limbs, as is known, which is the arousal in the body itself, which precisely brings to the depth of everything. So Knowledge is what brings to the depth of everything, that is, to the depth of Wisdom.

## Drawing Water: the Power of Lower Understanding

> And lower Understanding is to draw up, through it, from this depth hidden in this idea of the One who fills all worlds, from hiddenness into revelation. For because he takes in this grasp itself well, he can bring it into several matters set apart from it: as into several details of the existence of the created worlds, how they come into being from nothing to something, which is hidden in the general grasp.

That is: this is what is called gazing at the glory of the King, seeing the honor of the King: the more you appreciate, the more you look. And this is what we called lower Understanding: lower Understanding is to draw from the deep waters, to make it accessible. So from the depth you draw up the water. That is lower Understanding, which is the outcome: after contemplation, and after you have drawn from the very essence of the spring, it clothes it in new garments, until it touches the heart, and the heart is stirred by it. This is called drawing water, which is lower Understanding, as written above.

> And without this power of lower Understanding in this grasp of the One who fills all worlds, this grasp stays only in the mind of his understanding, and he will not know what to do with it, nor how to bring this contemplation into the quality of the bringing into being of something from nothing at all; as is understood from all the above; and the understanding will understand.

So he carries all this on within the parable of the One who fills all worlds and the bringing of creation into being, something from nothing; and it is not just a parable. This parable truly takes a central point; and so the whole book of the Gate of Unity takes the central ideas of Hasidic teaching and explains them to us, sets them out for us; and with all this, and through this, explains to us how to contemplate, and how to take every idea and understand every idea.

## Why Contemplation Is Held Back

The Rebbe concludes:

> And from this the reason is understood for what holds back the newcomers, or even the old-timers, who lack lower Understanding: even though they grasp very finely at the time they hear and see, they cannot bring the matter at all into contemplation in prayer, on the bringing into being of the worlds; until one asks, what is to be done with all this, and for what benefit is it needed?

Clearly. This is why people do not understand why to study Hasidic teaching, why to contemplate these things: because you heard an idea and it did not come down to stir the heart; and you did not contemplate it until you truly brought it out from potential into actuality. So truly, why do we need to know these ideas, which seemingly have nothing to do with us, in the higher worlds? And then one also asks what to do in prayer. In prayer we say words I have no bond to. The remedy for all this is the work of contemplation we have spoken of here.

> For in his mind they are two separate things, because it remains on the level of understanding alone.

It is as if they are two separate things: there is the idea I study, like, to make a distinction, mathematics; and it makes no difference to me, it does not change me; it is some knowledge in the world. To make a distinction.

> Separate, yes: only on the level of understanding alone in his mind, completely separate from every matter; and this is enough for one who understands.
