## The Essence of Contemplation

The first chapter. The text reads:

> Now, the essence of contemplation is the strong gaze into the depth of a matter, and standing upon it at length until one understands it thoroughly, in all its parts, in the finest details. It is the inwardness of Binah, called in the language of the Gemara iyun, study.

That is: to explain the foundation of the work of contemplation. Contemplation of the higher matters, of the ideas of Kabbalah and Chassidus, in the finest details, in order to awaken by this contemplation the soul, and the character traits in the soul, to arousal, as we explained in the previous kuntres, the Kuntres of Arousal, which explains how the soul is aroused and what that arousal is. This is the revelation of the soul, filling a person's thought, speech and action, and through this lighting up him and those around him with Godly light. The Godly light comes from the soul; it is revealed through study of the inwardness, which is a matter of the inner Torah. The parable for all this, for study and contemplation, is the flowing of water, as we will see.

> That is: reading through gives only the meaning of the matter at first glance, which goes on flowing quickly, without any stopping to stand on it at all. As is known, by way of parable: one who looks at something with his eye and does not contemplate it at all, its quality and its essence in all its inner and outer parts, but only in passing, will surely forget it entirely after a while; nor can he tell another anything but the general matter of what he saw with a passing glance, and not with a fixed gaze at all. So too, passing the eye of the mind over some reasoning, at first glance, without stopping to study it well: he will not know that reasoning to its end at all.

This means that contemplation through study of the details engraves the matter in the mind. Here is a parable: when a person sees someone or something in passing, it is like the surface of the river, only the outer part, and so there was no fixed gaze on all the details. So it is in study too: when we learn with a general overview, a few minutes after the class we cannot explain the details any more, because it was not engraved in the memory; the study did not stop and stay.

This shows that there is a power in the mind called the power of iyun, of study, and it is called the inwardness of Binah. And the inwardness of Binah is truly what engraves; and not only engraves, it truly hews out and uncovers the inwardness of the spring, the inwardness of the whole current of the mind, the flowing of the mochin, as we said. It is also a parable, and it is also exactly how the flowing of the mind works. So when, through the power of the inwardness of Binah, called contemplation, we reach the depth of the spring, the depth of the matter, there the very essence of the matter truly is, and from there the flowings flow by themselves. Unlike a mere surface overview, where you do not touch the flowings at all.

## Depth, Length and Breadth

He now explains this in detail:

> For there are three things in every matter of the intellect: depth, length and breadth.

That is: he now brings us a parable, truly a parallel, to the mind of a person. We began to touch on it: we said what iyun is, the power of study, the power to look into a matter; it is in the inwardness of Binah. These are ideas of Kabbalah, like many ideas in the Zohar, such as that the inwardness of Atik is the inwardness of Binah: through the inwardness of Binah the inwardness of Atik is revealed. We will see later that the inwardness of Atik is the flowing itself, the spring itself. A river has length, breadth and depth, and the depth of the river is truly the spring itself, from which the water flows up out of the deep, breaks through and rises; and that is truly the body of the river itself, which then spreads out in length and breadth. And in parallel to this, every flowing of the mind works the same way: there is the depth of the idea, from which all its other aspects flow, and they show themselves in length and breadth.

Back to the words of the text:

> Breadth is the explanation of the idea to every side, in many details, like the breadth of a river; and not the things as they are alone, like a narrow river.

You have a wide river and a narrow river. A narrow river you can even jump across, because it has few sides, few details. But a wide river, with a great expanse from one bank to the other, speaks of an idea with very, very many details: to understand even one piece of an idea at one level, you have very, very many details to put together.

> And the length of the river: length is the great descent of the understanding, clothing the idea in different parables, until bringing it within the grasp of a small child.

For a river that flows stretches out in length. This is the length of the river: how far you can take an idea and explain it in parables, clothe it in other ideas, until you can explain it even to a child. There is length and there is breadth.

The Rebbe continues:

> And depth is like the depth of the river, from which it widens, while in itself it is not wide at all; yet it is the main essence of the river, the main course of its running from its source. And the waters above it in height, and at the sides of the depth, are secondary to it; they are only its spreading out in strength to the sides, in breadth, in height, and in the length of its course.

So as we said of the river, there is more: the spring from which the water flows out of the earth, from the great deep beneath the earth, and is purified. This is living water, passing through the small veins in the earth until it wells up in the depth of the river; and from there it widens into length and breadth. So the Rebbe has now explained the whole matter. Now, when we say that an idea is deep, what does it mean that it is deep? These are all the effects of the power of study, which looks in, separates and clarifies. All these expressions are expressions explained in the wisdom of Kabbalah, about how the world comes to be.

## The Foundation of Chabad: the Inner Torah

So the mind is also light, and the light of the mind shows itself and shines from the soul until it is revealed in a person's thought, speech and action. And this is the whole matter of Shaar HaYichud: by contemplating the oneness of God in the higher worlds, in all their details, we awaken these levels in the mind. And this is of the inner Torah, for the Torah is "broader than the earth." All study of Torah, the revealed Torah too, is above time and place, until it can affect this world, for the Torah and the Holy One, blessed be He, are all one: He clothed Himself in the Torah. In studying the inner Torah you uncover the depth of the spring. In the parable here, all of the inner Torah is the depth within the spring. And in the depth there is length, breadth and depth, until you reach the essence of the spring itself, which is the inwardness of Atik, the inwardness of the mochin: as the mind flows from some level above the mind, which we said is the soul, which is above time and place. So the human mind is the bond between the spiritual worlds, which are the worlds of thought and beyond, and this material, physical world below, within time and place. A person, who is matter and has a mind, joins these two together. And so the parallel between the flowing of the mochin, of the mind, and the flowing of the river is not only a parallel: it is itself the way to uncover the inwardness of the river above, to uncover the inner Torah.

And the purpose of this holy work, called the work of contemplation, which is the foundation of the Chassidus of Chabad, is to fill the mind, the consciousness, with the contemplation of these Godly matters as they are above. And through this, the speech that awakens the person himself: then his thought, speech and action, in good deeds, are nullified to God. And then he reveals this oneness in the world too, for the world in itself darkens and hides the Godliness that brings it into being. And through contemplating all the finest details, including what we are doing now, a person contemplates within his own mind and sees how it runs parallel to the flowing of the mochin above. The more details in which he understands it, the more he sees the Godliness in the world itself; and this is to bring about the revelation of Godliness in the world.

And this is the change of consciousness: instead of the lawlessness, the hiddenness and the darkness of the material world, he sees within matter the soul that brings it into being, which is the Godliness that brings the world into being. This is what we said: the simple oneness of God. We clarify the oneness of all the worlds, and uncover all the levels of the ideas of Kabbalah, which are in the soul, as we said, and also above; and a person knows himself, and by knowing himself, "from my flesh I shall see God," he knows the higher worlds. Through this he lives in the awareness of the higher worlds even while he is in the world of matter, and balances the two. And this is truly a sobering, an opening of the eyes, to see the Godliness revealed to us within the world, which in itself seems separate. And this work of contemplation, which as a whole is also the service of prayer, is the foundation of the Chassidus of Chabad.

## The Depth Above and the Depth Below

The Rebbe continues:

> So the depth of the idea is the very essence of its point as it is, and it is called the depth of the idea. All that spreads out in explaining it, to every side, in great breadth, in many details; and in length, in the great descent; and also in height, to understand from it much more, above it, up to the depth of the height, like the rising of the waters.

Here the Rebbe already adds a note within the point. Let us first explain the point he makes. The depth of the idea is the very point of the idea. If you have caught the point, the very point of the idea, then everything you understand afterward will be right, also when you come to explain Chassidus, or anything in the Torah. If you have understood the very point, you can clothe it in the garments of different words and different letters, explain it differently to different people, and you will always be true to the truth, because you have grasped the very point. So the very point is the depth of the river, as we said: it is the depth of the idea, and all that spreads out afterward comes from it.

And then he widens it further: the depth of the idea, and the height, he gives here within the depth. Just as you can dive into the depth of the river, the depth of the idea, until you reach the point. But the depth below is bound to the depth above. He brings this here from Sefer Yetzirah, and it is here in brackets: up to the depth of the height, like the rising of the waters above at the time of the Flood, which is how it will be in the days of Mashiach, in the spiritual sense, in the six hundredth year of Noah's life. What is written here was written even before the year six hundred of Noah's life: the year six hundred corresponds to eighteen-forty. It was written very close to it, but before the year six hundred of Noah's life.

And what really happened then? Below, the movement of the Haskalah began; but there were also very great discoveries in science. From the middle of the nineteenth century, of course, the whole world of electricity and physics and the rest began. And in the inner Torah too: truly the revelation of the teaching of Chassidus, Chabad in particular, which reveals the whole inner Torah, and from there to our generation, in which the inner Torah has been revealed in every corner. There is still the inwardness of the inner Torah to reveal, which is its meaning, and that is what we are trying to do here.

So he brings what the Zohar says: as it was in the Flood in the days of Noah, there will be a flood of knowledge, in which both the springs above and the springs below will open. The springs of the heavens, of the material wisdom, the wisdom of this world, reach its depth; and through this, in parallel, and this too is a parable, we reach the spiritual wisdom, the wisdom above, the waters above, where the springs open too. We have the merit to be in the generation of the days of Mashiach: "For the earth shall be full of the knowledge of God, as the waters cover the sea." The waters are revealed from below and from above, spiritual knowledge and material knowledge, and both are revealed and become one, until humanity itself enters this level. That is the days of Mashiach: there will be no more foolishness, for knowledge and understanding have been revealed in the world, not only material understanding but spiritual understanding, which complete one another; and that in itself solves all the troubles of the exile. All this he hints at here, within the brackets, from what is written in Sefer Yetzirah. It says:

> "And the windows of heaven were opened": to draw down from the waters above the heavens, called the depth of the height, which depends on the depth below.

This he brings from Sefer Yetzirah. How do you reach, in our parable of the river, the waters above the river? It is as if in some spiritual mirror: as you sink deep down into the river, so above, in parallel, you rise higher. Yes, Sefer Yetzirah says that the depth of the height depends on the depth below. The deeper you know, the higher you also reach in the same measure, for "their beginning is fixed in their end," as it is written. There, at length, Sefer Yetzirah truly explains that in the Infinite, at the points of the Infinite, above and below are one.

The Rebbe brings a parable for this. When you see a whole building, that is the depth below: "last in deed, first in thought." When you see the finished deed, you see the embodiment of the first plan, the drawings of the building; and now you see it in fact. That is "last in deed, first in thought": in the first thought you had a picture of what was to be built, and when you see it built you understand the drawing too. This is a parable to understand what is above: everything you see in the physical, which is the finished deed, this physical, material world, and what you see imprinted in this world, is itself the plan that is above. And again he hints at the Torah: as it came down into this world to teach us a good and long life in the physical, in matter, in this world, its root is far, far above the world, and it shows itself in the way we live in this world. It truly reveals the higher plan, which is the Torah. So too in the mind: when you reach the deepest point in the mind, you also reach the highest point, from which the mind flows.

## A River Goes Out of Eden

In any case, he continues:

> Everything is drawn from the very depth of its point, as it flows from Chochmah, which is called the eye; or as it is written, "And a river went out of Eden."

This is the parable of the verse too. That is: a river going out of Eden is not just a parable. These ideas also run parallel to the flowing of the river: the thought that goes out of Eden, which is Chochmah. Chochmah is called ayin, nothing, and Binah is already called yesh, something. Why? When you have a thought that you feel and are aware of, it is something. But the root of the thought, before it was grasped, is nothing as far as you are concerned, because it is not yet grasped. Yet this nothing is truly the root, from which the something then shows itself. That is why wisdom "is found from nothing": something from nothing. As in "a river went out of Eden," and as it says in tractate Berachot, "No eye has seen, O God, but You": Eden, which cannot be grasped, is nothing; yet from there the river goes out.

In the Rebbe's words, Binah is called a river and Chochmah is called a spring, as we have now explained at length: Binah is when there is already length, breadth and depth, a river; there is a thought. In the very highest worlds too, Binah is the root, far, far above what there was before the world was, down to our own Binah, which is truly our power of thought. So it goes out of the spring. The spring is the root from which the water flows: the flowings of the mind, which flow without stopping, thank God, from the eye of Chochmah into Binah. And Chochmah is called a spring, as is known.

## The Fiftieth Gate: Holding the Thought Still

> And it is called iyun, study: one stands on the way of the idea and looks into it at length.

So the power of the inwardness of Binah: in the language of Kabbalah and in Sefer Yetzirah, as we said, there are thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom and fifty gates of understanding. The first gate of Binah is called the inwardness of Binah, contemplation, where you stop and dig with this power, the power of study, which stands still. It is close to the first gate from Chochmah: that is the fiftieth gate, the gate of nun. The fiftieth gate is study. That is why the word hitbonenut, contemplation, has two letters nun; this too is hinted at and explained by the Rebbe: contemplation with two nuns is the matter of the fiftieth gate. The fiftieth gate is the power of study, from which new ideas flow; for beyond the fiftieth gate you are already in Chochmah: you have passed the river and entered Eden.

And what is it, in the fiftieth gate? To stand on the way of the idea and look into it very much; this is holding back, the opposite of speed. From Binah onward the river flows quickly, the thought flows quickly. But to hold the thought back, before all its consequences, before all the details and explanations that run with the letters of thought; to stop on the very point and look into it there, until the matter takes shape broadly.

> That is: to come to the depth of the idea as it is in its very inwardness, and precisely in its innermost core; like one who looks at something with his eye, not in passing, but with a watchful, fixed gaze.

So this is iyun too, from the word ayin, the eye: you look, you look into something, you demand of the eye that it take in the depth of the matter, in all its details. And this is the eye in thought, which looks in and demands of the eye a deep, fixed gaze, to reach the depth of the idea. And this is the beauty of this kuntres: it also explains to us how to treat our own world of thought, which is the foundation of all Torah study, revealed and hidden. And through this it uncovers the powers of our thought, which run parallel to the powers of the worlds, or rather to the lights in the higher worlds and their revelation, which explains the whole wisdom of Kabbalah.

> And it is called hitbonenut, contemplation, precisely with a double nun: that is, one contemplates and looks into it at length, as Rashi explains the word iyun: to stand on the matter and understand it thoroughly. So this study is only a vessel for the deepening of Binah itself.

Now, this study is only a deepening: you look in, the eye that is in Binah. That is, the inwardness of Binah: the study is only there to bring a greater widening in Binah afterward.

> For seemingly, this study is what the world calls depth of mind.

Depth of mind is also a kind of divine inspiration, among the Chassidim since the Baal Shem Tov: to reach depth of mind. And also derhern, which is a kind of hearing, of listening: "the breath of the Almighty gives them understanding." It is a kind of revelation of divine inspiration. This is not mentioned here explicitly, but it is the inwardness of the matter: when you study the inwardness and reach the depth of the idea, from there come new insights in Torah.

## Higher Even Than Chochmah

> But it is not so, depth of mind; that is, it is only a vessel for coming to the very depth of the matter. It is above that; it is called going deep, which reaches above even Chochmah itself.

And here he says: what the world calls depth of mind is a byword for divine inspiration. But here he says that this study is truly not the result of depth of mind, as the world thinks: it is above depth of mind, above even divine inspiration, to reach the depth of the idea. What is called going deep reaches above even Chochmah itself; even though Chochmah "is found from nothing," and cannot be grasped until it is clothed in Binah, and through study, in the inwardness of Binah. This is one of the explanations in the Zohar: that the inwardness of Binah is the inwardness of Atik. The inwardness of Atik is the inwardness of the inwardness of Keter, the crown. It is the level that will be revealed only in the time to come, in the days of Mashiach; but it is present in everything, for everything has a root, and when we reach that root, it is above even Chochmah. That is, through contemplation in the inner Torah.

And the level explained here is above even divine inspiration. Divine inspiration is when some understanding that reaches above the mind clothes itself in the mind. Here you reach the depth of the essence, the depth of the matter, as it is above even spiritual, intellectual understanding: the matter as it is in itself. And this is so in the revealed Torah and in the inner Torah alike.

> It reaches above even Chochmah itself. For though Chochmah is the very eye of the idea, before it comes to the depth of the point of the idea in Binah, as a spring is to a river, as said above; the deepening in the root of the idea reaches, in its root, to the root that draws down the flowing of the spring of Chochmah, and it is called the depths of wisdom, or the hidden things of wisdom.
