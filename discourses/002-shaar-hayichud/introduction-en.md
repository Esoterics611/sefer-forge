## What This Work Is

A short introduction to the Gate of Unity. The Mitteler Rebbe writes in his own introduction:

> The essence of this treatise speaks of the unity of Godliness in every single detail of the chain of worlds, and of the ten divine attributes in the worlds of Emanation, Creation, Formation and Action. It is a summary of all that a person can bring close to the intellect and to understanding, clothed and imprinted in the mind and the heart, in every detail of the chain of worlds, from the first contraction to the end of the world of action. And it is called by the name "the Treatise on Contemplation."

Let us explain this a little. It is known that the Gate of Unity is the key to the Hasidic teaching of Chabad. The Mitteler Rebbe was the son of the author of the Tanya, the Alter Rebbe; and as he testifies, the author of the Tanya gave his soul to establish the simple unity of God in the heart of every Jew, through contemplation, and so through the study of Torah.

This is the study of the inner Torah, the reasons of the commandments. The revealed Torah, the Torah, the Talmud and the Code of Jewish Law, makes clear what is the right deed to do in the world of action. The inner Torah belongs more to the world of thought: what is the right intention in all our deeds, the intention in the commandments, and the foundation of all Jewish life, which is the service of prayer, where the main thing is the intention. Prayer also has its practical side, which the Code of Jewish Law writes about, and its inner side, which belongs to the inner Torah; and of course everything is bound together. And so the thought in prayer is truly part of the doing too.

## Contemplation, the Foundation of Chabad

In general, the whole of Hasidic teaching truly explains prayer. And there is the inwardness of every intention, in Torah, in all the commandments, and in the human soul, which is truly the work on the character. There is the service of prayer, the service of the commandments, the service of Torah study, and there is also the work of refining the character, which is the inwardness of all of them. And refining the character is entirely a matter of contemplation.

For contemplation means this: the mind looks into a matter and concentrates on it, detail by detail. That is why these books are long and flow with very many explanations and commentaries, and with branchings too: into one sentence there may enter parts of pages of roots, like pages in which the spring flows up and sinks back, like a river. For this contemplation, with all its details, awakens the arousal in the heart.

This work, called the Gate of Unity, is the second treatise. When the Mitteler Rebbe wrote his explanation, the first treatise was called the Treatise on Arousal. Its purpose was to explain the ways of arousal in the mind and heart and thought that are born of contemplation, in the order of the five levels of the soul, from the soul that gives life to the body, through the spirit and the higher soul, up to the living essence and the single, innermost essence: whatever concerns the body and the matters of arousal. That is, the Treatise on Arousal explains the ways in which the arousal in the mind and in the heart comes from contemplation.

And I have come to explain here, in this introduction to the Gate of Unity, where we see that the words are very concentrated and the matters very deep, to open a door to understanding the work of contemplation, which is truly the essence of the Hasidic teaching of Chabad. And here is an example of what we see: there is arousal, and there is contemplation. The arousal does not come from us, but from above. The arousal in the mind and the heart is a revelation of the five levels of the soul. And when we begin to explain the levels of the human soul, a person begins to know himself through contemplation, as it says, "From my flesh I shall see God." So the very structure of the human soul, the garments of thought, speech and action, and the structures of the soul itself, run fully parallel to the structure of all the worlds. As the soul fills the body, so the Holy One, blessed be He, fills the world.

## Wisdom, Understanding and the Structure of the Soul

We need to understand all the details of the distinctions between the levels of the mind, Wisdom, Understanding and Knowledge, and of the levels called Israel the Elder and lower Understanding, in the service of the heart; and what branches out from them into actual deeds, which is the whole of a person. The whole root of the unfolding of a person's thought: we go into a person's thought, into the finest details of all its levels, from the nothingness that is above thought down to all the levels we will explain further on, with God's help. They are included in Wisdom, Understanding and Knowledge, and in Israel the Elder and lower Understanding, which are the powers of the mind among the ten divine attributes; and we will set them out through the whole discourse, in two dimensions: one as they are in the higher worlds, the other as they are in the human soul.

In the human soul this is truly the process from the soul to its first stirrings in a person's mind, which is Wisdom and Understanding. Wisdom is a flash of lightning, and Understanding is already a breadth in which that flashing lightning is clothed: with all its potential, yet all of it in a state that is not yet felt. Then, in the mind of Understanding, understanding one thing from another, this point of Wisdom takes on length and breadth and depth. And the lowest level of Understanding is when it clothes itself in letters, the letters of thought, which are called the Kingship of lower Understanding. Lower Understanding is truly the outer level, the lower level of Understanding. It is like a womb, where the point itself spreads out into these three directions, depth, breadth and length, which correspond to a spiritual space, for space is three axes.

So in our soul too: before I think something, when there is an idea, there are many things in the soul that are on the level of Wisdom, hidden but able to be revealed. For example: when you think about something, at that moment your mind is clothed in one thought. You may know a thousand other subjects; they are there in some power and potential, but your mind is clothed now in another thought. So they are hidden. That is the point of Wisdom. When you take a point and go deep into it, that is contemplation and study. In Hebrew, the word for study comes from the word for the eye, which is also the eye of depth, for you look into the matter with contemplation; and the word for contemplation comes from the word for Understanding.

So this point, which is above the revealed intellect, takes on the dimensions of length, breadth and depth, until it can be clothed in letters of thought. And there are very, very many levels in them too: there are thoughts that flow like a river, most of which you are not aware of; down to the lowest level, which is the Kingship of lower Understanding. That is already speech too, but a speech that is still very much within thought. The letters of thought, when we think in a way we are aware of, are already the level of Leah, the feminine side of the Small Countenance.

Let us go on to the powers of the mind. All of Hasidic teaching, with all of Kabbalah, speaks of this: the union of Wisdom and Understanding, which is truly the union of the powers of the mind. And through contemplating Godliness, whatever you contemplate, we take the things that are hidden within us, down to what is hidden in the soul, down to the hidden that cannot be revealed and the hidden that can be revealed, which are the levels of all the things that are within us; and through contemplation you develop them. And this is the matter of actual deeds: instead of contemplating unwanted things, which lead to unwanted opinions and speech, to contemplate Godly things, good things that bring self-nullification within us. And against this, the same measure shines from above: "A spirit draws a spirit, and draws down a spirit." It awakens the same things above as well.

## Hidden Worlds and Revealed Worlds: Hear, O Israel

To understand how study and contemplation of Godliness bring all of this about, let us picture it. In Hebrew, the word for study also comes from the word for a spring, as in "Your springs shall spread outward." This spring is the flowing of the powers of the mind, of Wisdom, Understanding and Knowledge, from the Understanding of the Father and the Mother. All contemplation of Godliness, which is contemplation of exactly the things spoken of here, in every detail of how creation comes to be and of the Godly levels there: we will understand how this contemplation, called study, brings about all of the above. What is the actual deed, which is the whole of a person? The service of the heart is the result of study and contemplation; and the service of the heart is what makes a person eager to keep the whole Torah and all the commandments, the two hundred and forty-eight positive commandments and the three hundred and sixty-five prohibitions, and all of them are awakened by the intention.

That is why the commandment to know Godliness is said precisely with the word "hear," a word of understanding, which is the study described above. This is clearly the meaning of "Hear, O Israel." What is "hear"? It is study, a word of understanding. In Yiddish there is a word for hearing that means perceiving something inwardly, and it is a byword for divine inspiration: hearing here is when you understand something, until you study the matter so deeply that new currents flow, new thoughts, new powers and new insights. That is what is called understanding and study. And that is what is called "hear": the commandment "Hear, O Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One." This oneness is the intention of all the commandments, of the whole of their lives. And this hearing is the study we are speaking of here, which is also the matter of the Gate of Unity: study of the oneness of God.

It is generally known that there are two kinds of worlds: hidden worlds and revealed worlds. The hidden worlds are the world of Emanation, called the unity of the cause, the unity of what is emanated with the One who emanates it. The revealed worlds are the three worlds of Creation, Formation and Action, which are drawn from the Kingship of the world of Emanation, as it is written, "Your kingship is the kingship of all worlds."

A person also has the level of revealed worlds: that is already thought, speech and action. Thought, thought in letters, is already a revealed world; it is already revealed. And all the deeper layers of the soul are in the hidden worlds. There is always a complete parallel here between the explanation of the human soul and the revelation of the Holy One, blessed be He, in the worlds: they correspond to each other.

And just as within a person there are wellsprings, springs of inner flowing that are in the hidden worlds, they open only when you awaken them by contemplating deeply. As you know: when you contemplate something, you go deep, and suddenly you give many of the powers of your soul to that matter. That lights it up; you can go inside. But if you give it only a general glance, out of the corner of your eye, you have not truly seen the essence of the details. So it is with Godliness: the more you contemplate, the more you open the matter and open new springs. And because this concerns the inwardness of your own self, from there these flowings will show themselves, of themselves, in thought, speech and action that are good. This is the intention: you contemplate the oneness of God until it wakes you, until it goes in.

## The Intentions of Kabbalah and of Hasidic Teaching

And the Hasidic teaching of Chabad came, and especially the Gate of Unity and the discourses of the Mitteler Rebbe, to lay foundations for every single person to enter within the holy. And this is in two dimensions. One is knowing the worlds. But it is not mere knowledge: it is knowledge in which a person's knowing himself is the foundation of all the work on the character. And from it the service of prayer and all these things are lit up. For in prayer, the hints hinted at in the prayer, and the intentions that come for the sake of God: the intentions of Hasidic teaching in prayer run parallel to the descriptions of the intentions of Kabbalah.

The Kabbalists focus their intentions on holy names that hint at lofty matters in the higher worlds, and they direct themselves there. That intention is a kind of contemplation. Contemplation in Hasidic teaching is of the same kind; but instead of contemplating the details and the names, one contemplates in more general meanings and more abstract ideas. It is more a matter of a person understanding himself and seeing the revelation of Godliness.

The service of the intentions of Kabbalah is, so to speak, a technical service: to act and to bring about unions in the higher worlds. The intention of prayer in Hasidic teaching is to contemplate the structures of the higher worlds in order to awaken love and longing in the soul, awakened and revealed in the mind and the heart, until arousal and the revelation of the love of God in prayer: love and awe. And to come to good resolutions; for the Hasidic intention in prayer strips a person of the garments of this world's busy thoughts, through deep contemplation of the details we will study here.

Although both the intentions of Kabbalah and the intentions of Hasidic teaching speak of the same divine attributes and the same worlds, in Kabbalah one does particular and general work in the higher worlds, and in Hasidic teaching one contemplates the higher worlds in order to awaken love and awe in the heart and to affect the character traits in the heart. That is why the teaching of Kabbalah is for a chosen few: to direct one's intentions and act in the higher worlds needs purity of soul, holiness and very great awe, and all the more so to know the intentions in their depth; and it is truly a tremendous level in the study of Torah. The Hasidic intentions are for every single person, each by his own level: everyone can contemplate, and be awakened to love and awe, from those same matters.

## An Opening to the Work of Contemplation

This is the essence of the treatise: the whole of the Gate of Unity comes to raise up the strength of Israel and to light up their eyes; and first of all, the world of contemplation, and especially contemplation in prayer. In the chapters before us we will see what contemplation is: how it is in a person, until we look inward and see how the structure of our soul runs parallel to the revelation of the higher worlds, and that our thought is bound to something higher. And when we contemplate the finest details, we awaken the hidden love, which is the foundation of the whole book of the Tanya. And the love comes twice: "And you shall love," twice, with the light; these are the two lights, the returning light and the direct light, which awaken above.

As the Rebbe brings in his prayer book in the name of the Maggid: "Know what is above you: know that all that is above, all of it is from you." So may it be for us.
